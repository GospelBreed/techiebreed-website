Most Nigerian businesses already have their best marketing list. It is sitting in the phone: hundreds or thousands of people who have asked a price, placed an order, booked an appointment or paid for something. A well-timed WhatsApp message to the right part of that list can bring back repeat customers faster than almost any ad.

It can also go badly wrong. Send the same promo to everyone, every few days, to people who never asked for it, and you will see the blocks and reports pile up. Meta can restrict your number, customers start ignoring you, and under Nigerian data protection law you can end up on the wrong side of a complaint.

This guide shows you how to send WhatsApp broadcast messages for your business in Nigeria the right way: how to build a list people actually agreed to, which of WhatsApp's three sending routes fits your size, how to use AI to write messages people respond to, and how to measure whether a broadcast made you money.

## Why most WhatsApp broadcasts fail

When a broadcast flops, the message is rarely the only problem. Usually it is one of these:

**The list was never permission-based.** Contacts saved from a market day, numbers copied from a group, or worse, a "database of active WhatsApp users" bought from someone online. People who don't know you or don't remember agreeing to hear from you are the ones who block and report.

**Everyone gets everything.** A customer who bought baby clothes two years ago receives the same men's shoe promo as someone who enquired yesterday. Irrelevant messages train people to mute you.

**Too often, with no way out.** No "reply STOP" line, no record of who asked to stop, and the same people get messaged again next week.

**Nobody is ready for the replies.** A broadcast is only half the job. If 40 people reply "How much?" and nobody answers until tomorrow, most of the value of the send is lost.

**No measurement.** Without a way to link a broadcast to orders, you can't tell a profitable message from a noisy one, so you keep repeating whatever feels busy.

Fix those five and the technology choice becomes much easier.

## The three ways to send WhatsApp broadcasts in 2026

WhatsApp now gives businesses three official routes. They differ in who you can reach, how many people, what it costs and how much you can measure.

### 1. Broadcast lists (WhatsApp and WhatsApp Business app)

The familiar option. You create a list, add contacts and send one message that each person receives as a private chat. Two limits catch people out: a list holds up to 256 contacts, and the message only reaches people who have saved your number in their phone. If a customer hasn't saved you, it simply never arrives, with no error.

There is also a newer limit. In March 2025, [TechCrunch reported](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/18/whatsapp-will-soon-limit-number-of-broadcast-messages-users-and-businesses-can-send/) that WhatsApp would start testing a monthly cap on broadcast messages to curb spam. That cap has since been rolling out gradually by country. Where it applies to your account, the app shows how many broadcasts you have left under Settings, then Broadcasts.

Broadcast lists still suit a small business with a few hundred loyal customers who already have the number saved.

### 2. Business broadcasts (inside the WhatsApp Business app)

Meta has added a more capable option to the Business app. According to [WhatsApp's own page on business broadcasts](https://whatsappbusiness.com/resources/resource-library/business-broadcasts-whatsapp/), you find it under Tools, choose an audience, add call-to-action buttons (such as book, order or call), send now or schedule for later, and then see insights such as read rate and reply rate. Importantly, Meta says these messages reach customers even if they haven't saved your number.

Business broadcasts are a paid feature where they have launched, charged per delivered message, and availability is still rolling out by country. If you don't see business broadcasts under Tools, it hasn't reached your account yet. Check the price the app shows before you confirm a send rather than relying on figures quoted elsewhere, because pricing varies by the recipient's country.

### 3. Marketing messages on the WhatsApp Business Platform (API)

This is the route for larger lists, several staff, and anything connected to your other systems. You send pre-approved **marketing templates** through a provider or the Cloud API, and Meta charges per delivered message at a rate set for the recipient's country. (Our guide to [WhatsApp AI assistants in Nigeria](/insights/whatsapp-ai-assistant-for-business-nigeria) explains the App vs Platform decision and the 1 October 2026 pricing changes in more detail.)

Two limits matter here, both from Meta's developer documentation:

- **Messaging limits.** According to [Meta's messaging limits documentation](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/messaging-limits), a new business portfolio can message 250 unique people outside a customer service window in any moving 24-hour period. Verifying your business is one way to move to 2,000, and after that Meta raises the limit automatically if your message quality stays high and you use at least half your current limit over seven days.
- **Per-user marketing limits.** [Meta may limit](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/templates/marketing-templates/per-user-limits/) how many marketing templates a person receives from all businesses when they are unlikely to engage. A blocked send returns error 131049, and Meta asks you to wait at least 24 hours before trying that person again. In plain terms: people who never read your messages will stop receiving them.

### Which route should you use?

A simple rule of thumb:

- **Under a few hundred engaged customers, one person handling chats:** broadcast lists, done well.
- **A growing list, customers who haven't saved your number, and you want buttons, scheduling and read rates:** business broadcasts in the app, if available to you.
- **Thousands of contacts, several staff, or messages triggered by your orders, bookings or payments:** the Business Platform through a provider or a custom build.

Whatever you choose, the rules on permission are the same.

## How to send WhatsApp broadcasts step by step

### Step 1: Build an opted-in list from people who already know you

WhatsApp's [Business Messaging Policy](https://business.whatsapp.com/policy) is direct about this: you may only contact people who have given you their number **and** have opted in to hear from you. Meta's own best-practice guidance goes further and suggests getting opt-in for each category of message, so someone who agreed to order updates hasn't automatically agreed to promotions.

Practical places to ask, using customers you already have:

- **At checkout or on the receipt:** "Want new arrivals and offers on WhatsApp? Reply YES."
- **On your website and Instagram bio:** a wa.me link with a pre-filled message such as "Please add me to your weekly offers" so the opt-in arrives in writing.
- **In-store or at events:** a QR code beside the till that opens the same wa.me link.
- **In an ongoing chat:** after you've helped someone, ask once whether they'd like occasional offers.

Keep a simple record of who opted in, when, how and for what. A spreadsheet column is enough to start. That record is what protects you if a customer later says they never agreed.

What not to do: never buy a "WhatsApp database", never scrape numbers from groups, and don't add people to a broadcast just because they once asked a question.

### Step 2: Segment before you write anything

Even a small list splits naturally into groups that want different things:

- **Past buyers**, by what they bought and when
- **Enquirers who didn't buy**, by what they asked about
- **Location**, if you deliver or run branches in different cities
- **Customer value**, for example repeat buyers versus first-time buyers

In the Business app, labels are the simplest way to do this. On the Platform, your CRM or spreadsheet holds these fields. Three well-chosen segments will usually beat one big blast.

### Step 3: Write messages people are glad to get

A good broadcast is short, relevant and easy to act on. A useful pattern:

1. **Who it's from and why they're getting it** ("Hi Bisi, it's Adaeze Fabrics, you bought ankara from us in August")
2. **One clear piece of value** (new stock in their size, a restock of what they asked about, a time-limited offer)
3. **One action** (a button, a link, or "reply 1 to reserve")
4. **An easy way out** ("Reply STOP to stop offers")

Put the real price in the message if it's about a product. "DM for price" frustrates customers and wastes the replies you worked to get. Our guide to [automating Instagram DMs](/insights/automate-instagram-dms-nigeria) covers why that pattern backfires on social media too.

### Step 4: Use AI to draft, adapt and check, not to decide

This is where AI saves real time for a small team. Used well, a general AI assistant can help you:

- **Draft three versions** of the same offer for different segments from one brief ("write for past buyers, for enquirers who didn't buy, and for first-time buyers").
- **Adapt the tone** to how your customers actually speak, including Nigerian Pidgin, then have a fluent person on your team check it before it goes out.
- **Shorten** a long message to the few lines people will read on a phone.
- **Summarise replies** after a send, so you can see the common questions and objections in a minute instead of an hour.

A simple brief gets better results than "write a WhatsApp promo": give the AI your product, the exact price, who the segment is, what they bought before, the one action you want and the opt-out line, and ask for three short options under 60 words.

Keep a person responsible for the final message. AI tools can state a wrong price, invent a deadline or promise a discount you didn't approve. Every figure in a broadcast should come from you, not from the model. Learning to brief, edit and fact-check AI output like this is a core part of our Academy's **Business Growth with Digital Marketing & AI** programme, where participants practise on their own business's real campaigns.

### Step 5: Send, pace and watch the signals

- **Start small.** Send to your most engaged segment first. If replies look good and blocks are low, widen it.
- **Pick your timing.** Schedule for when your customers usually chat with you, which your own chat history will show.
- **Don't over-send.** There is no universal "right" frequency, but if a segment stops reading, send to it less. Meta's per-user limits will reduce delivery to unengaged people anyway.
- **Watch quality.** On the Platform, check your quality rating in WhatsApp Manager after each campaign. A run of blocks is a signal to change the message or the audience, not to send harder.

### Step 6: Be ready for the replies

Plan who answers, during which hours, before you press send. On the Platform, a customer's reply opens a 24-hour customer service window where your team can respond freely, so fast replies matter. Automation helps here: tag replies by product, route them to the right person, and send an instant acknowledgement with prices and a payment link for the common questions. A [WhatsApp AI assistant](/insights/whatsapp-ai-assistant-for-business-nigeria) can take that first layer while handing serious buyers to a person.

### Step 7: Honour every opt-out, every time

Under section 36 of the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023, a person can object at any time to their data being used for direct marketing, and once they do, it must no longer be used for that purpose. WhatsApp's policy says the same: respect every request to stop, on or off WhatsApp.

Make this automatic wherever you can. When someone replies STOP, remove them from every marketing segment the same day and record the date. If you send email or SMS too, the opt-out should reach those lists as well. Our guide to [email marketing for small businesses in Nigeria](/insights/email-marketing-small-business-nigeria) covers consent records in more detail.

### Step 8: Measure each broadcast in naira

Give every broadcast a way to be traced: a unique offer code, a dedicated payment link, or a pre-filled wa.me message per campaign. Then track four numbers per send: messages delivered, replies, orders, and revenue. Cost per sale matters too if you're paying per message. ## A real cautionary case from Nigeria

Unwanted marketing messages are not just a reputational risk. In February 2025, the Federal High Court in Abuja ordered Eat'n'Go, operator of Domino's Pizza in Nigeria, to pay ₦3 million in damages to a customer, Chukwunweike Akosa Araka, over unsolicited marketing messages, as [reported by Technext](https://technext24.com/news/nigerian-won-data-privacy-case-dominos/) and [Techpoint Africa](https://techpoint.africa/news/dominos-pizza-to-pay-3m/). According to the reports, his details had been shared for order delivery through a food delivery platform, he later began receiving marketing messages, and they continued after he asked for them to stop.

Those messages were SMS rather than WhatsApp, but the principle carries over directly: data collected to deliver an order doesn't automatically give you permission to market to that person, and a request to stop must be honoured. Build your broadcast process around both.

## Mistakes to avoid

**Using unofficial bulk-sender tools.** Browser extensions and modified WhatsApp apps that promise unlimited sends operate outside WhatsApp's terms. The Business Messaging Policy allows Meta to limit or remove access for messaging people at scale in an unauthorised manner, so the "saving" can cost you the number.

**Adding customers to groups without asking.** A group exposes every member's number to everyone else and is not a substitute for a permission-based broadcast.

**Splitting one big list into many 256-contact lists.** It works around the list size, not the permission problem, and it does nothing about the monthly cap where it applies.

**Letting AI send unchecked.** Every price, date and promise should be confirmed by a person before it goes out.

**Measuring by "it felt busy".** Busy chats aren't sales. Track orders and revenue per broadcast.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many people can I add to a WhatsApp broadcast list?

Up to 256 contacts per list, and only people who have saved your number will receive it. For bigger audiences, or people who haven't saved your number, look at business broadcasts in the Business app (where available) or marketing templates on the WhatsApp Business Platform.

### Why didn't some customers receive my broadcast?

On a broadcast list, the most common reason is that they haven't saved your number. On the Business Platform, a send can fail if you've reached your messaging limit or if Meta's per-user marketing limit applies to that person (error 131049).

### Is sending WhatsApp broadcasts free?

Broadcast lists are free, subject to the monthly cap in countries where it has rolled out. Business broadcasts in the Business app are paid per delivered message where available, and marketing templates on the Business Platform are charged per delivered message at your recipients' country rate, plus any provider fees.

### Is it legal to send WhatsApp marketing messages in Nigeria?

Yes, when the people you message have agreed to receive them and you stop as soon as they ask. The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 gives people the right to object to direct marketing at any time, and WhatsApp's own policy requires opt-in. If your business handles sensitive data or sends at large scale, get proper legal advice.

### How often should a business send WhatsApp broadcasts?

There is no fixed number. Send when you have something genuinely useful for a specific segment, watch read and reply rates, and send less to anyone who stops engaging.

### Can I automate WhatsApp broadcasts?

Yes, on the Business Platform you can trigger messages from events in your systems, such as a restock, a birthday or a lapsed customer, and automate opt-out handling. Scheduling is also available in business broadcasts in the app. Keep a person in charge of what gets sent and to whom.
