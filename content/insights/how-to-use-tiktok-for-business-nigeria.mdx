Most Nigerian business owners already know their customers are on TikTok. The harder question is what to do about it. You post a few videos, one does well, the comments fill with "how much?" and "where are you located?", and a week later you have no idea whether any of it turned into a sale.

That is the gap this guide is about. Plenty of articles explain how to make money on TikTok as a creator, or how to pay for TikTok ads with a dollar card. Very few explain how a small business should use TikTok as part of how it actually sells: setting up the account properly, making videos it can keep producing, getting viewers into a conversation, following up, and measuring which videos bring in paying customers.

This is a practical, step-by-step guide on how to use TikTok for business in Nigeria, written for owners and marketers who want to do it themselves.

## Why TikTok deserves a place in your marketing plan

According to DataReportal's [Digital 2026: Nigeria](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-nigeria) report, TikTok's own advertising tools showed **47.8 million users aged 18 and above in Nigeria in late 2025**, equal to 38.2% of all adults and up 14.5 million on the previous year. The same report put Facebook's ad reach at 38.0 million and Instagram's at 10.0 million.

Two cautions before you read too much into that. DataReportal itself warns that ad audience figures are not the same as active users, and the TikTok figure covers adults only while the Meta figures do not specify an age range, so they are not perfectly comparable. Still, the direction is clear: for many consumer businesses, a large share of potential customers now spend time on TikTok, and it rewards short, real, useful videos more than polished adverts.

What TikTok does not do on its own is take orders, answer questions or chase people who said "I'll get back to you". That is why the rest of this guide connects TikTok to the rest of your sales process.

## Step 1: Set up a TikTok Business Account properly

Start by switching your profile to a Business Account in the TikTok app (under Settings and privacy, then Account) and choosing the category closest to what you sell. A Business Account gives you analytics on your videos and audience and access to business features such as Promote and the TikTok Ads Manager.

Three things to sort out at the start:

**Music.** TikTok's help centre is explicit that "businesses cannot use the general music library for commercial usage". Business content, including organic posts, ads and branded content, should use the [Commercial Music Library](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article?aid=10005905), a free library of pre-cleared tracks. In practice this means many trending songs will not be available to you. Plan around it: voiceovers, original sounds and commercial tracks work perfectly well for business content.

**Your bio and link.** Write one clear line about what you sell and where you deliver, then add a way to reach you. TikTok's rules for clickable website links in bios differ by account type and region and have changed over time. Guides such as [Buffer's](https://buffer.com/library/how-to-put-a-link-in-tiktok-bio/) report that personal accounts generally need 1,000 followers, while business accounts can unlock the link sooner depending on regional requirements. If the link field is available to you, use a WhatsApp link (wa.me/234...) with a pre-filled message, or a simple page that lists your WhatsApp, prices and delivery areas. If it is not yet available, put your WhatsApp number in the bio text and say "Send TIKTOK on WhatsApp".

**One owner, shared access.** Register the account with a business email you control, not a staff member's personal phone. If you plan to run ads, create a TikTok Business Center so you can give team members or an agency access without handing over your login.

## Step 2: Decide what your TikTok is for

"Go viral" is not a business goal. Before you film anything, pick one main job for TikTok over the next 30 days:

- More WhatsApp enquiries for a specific product or service
- More bookings for a slot-based service (salon, clinic, training, events)
- More people visiting a physical shop or market stall
- Building trust with buyers who have never bought from you

Then choose three or four types of video you can repeat every week. For most Nigerian small businesses, these work:

1. **The product or service in action.** How a cake is decorated, how a hair install is done, how an order is packed and dispatched.
2. **Answers to real questions.** Take the questions from your DMs and comments ("Do you deliver to Abuja?", "How long does it last?") and answer one per video.
3. **Behind the scenes.** The workshop, the kitchen, the team, the market run at 6am. People buy from people they feel they know.
4. **Proof.** Real customer reactions and reviews, shared with permission.

## Step 3: Make videos you can keep making

The businesses that do well on TikTok are rarely the ones with the best cameras. They are the ones that post consistently. A phone, daylight and a clear first two seconds will take you a long way.

A simple structure for most business videos:

- **Hook (first 1–2 seconds):** show the result or say the problem. "This is what ₦15,000 of small chops looks like."
- **Body (10–40 seconds):** show the process, answer the question or demonstrate the product.
- **Next step:** tell people exactly what to do. "Send SMALLCHOPS on WhatsApp, link in bio." Show the price where you can; vague "DM for price" posts waste conversations.

Film several videos in one session. An hour on a quiet afternoon can give you a week of content.

### Where AI helps (and where it doesn't)

AI tools are useful for the slow parts: brainstorming video ideas from your customer questions, writing hook options, drafting short scripts and shot lists, and writing captions. Our guide on [how to use AI to create social media content](/insights/ai-social-media-content-small-business-nigeria) covers the brief-plan-draft-review routine in detail, and it applies to TikTok too.

TikTok also offers its own AI tools for advertisers. [Symphony Creative Studio](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/symphony-creative-studio), which TikTok says is available to all logged-in TikTok for Business users, can generate video ads, scripts, avatar videos and translations. Treat its output as a draft. Customers buying from a small business want to see the real product, the real shop and real people.

Two rules to follow whenever you use AI:

- **Label realistic AI content.** TikTok [requires creators to label](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/ai-generated-content) AI-generated content that contains realistic images, audio or video, and may remove unlabelled AI content that breaks its guidelines. If you use an AI voiceover or AI avatar, switch on the AI-generated label.
- **Never use AI to fake the product.** An AI-made video of a dress, room or dish you do not actually sell misleads buyers, damages trust and leads to complaints.

## Step 4: Turn viewers into conversations

A video that gets views but no messages has done half its job. Make the next step easy and consistent:

- **One call to action per video,** always pointing to the same place, usually WhatsApp.
- **A keyword per campaign** ("Send TIKTOK10", "Send BRIDAL") so you can tell which videos produced which chats.
- **Reply to comments fast,** and use TikTok's reply-with-video feature for the questions many people ask. Each answer becomes new content.
- **Pin a "how to order" video** to the top of your profile with prices, delivery areas and payment options.

When the chats start arriving, someone has to answer them. Speed matters: a buyer who asks at 9pm and hears nothing until noon has probably bought elsewhere. A WhatsApp Business account with a greeting message, quick replies and labels for new, quoted and paid customers is the minimum. If enquiries pile up, a [WhatsApp AI assistant](/insights/whatsapp-ai-assistant-for-business-nigeria) can answer common questions instantly and hand serious buyers to a person.

## Step 5: Put money behind what already works

Once a video has done well organically, paying to show it to more of the right people is usually the safest first ad spend. You have two main routes.

**Promote (in the app).** Promote lets you boost an existing video from the TikTok app with a budget and duration. TikTok's help centre says Promote can be set up to bring messages into your TikTok inbox and, for "select customers and regions", into external apps including WhatsApp ([source](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/how-to-set-up-promote-for-other-messaging-apps?lang=en)). Check in the app whether the WhatsApp option is offered for your account. Promote only targets users aged 18 and over.

**TikTok Ads Manager.** For more control, use Ads Manager. Two campaign types suit small businesses:

- **Lead generation with Instant Forms.** People fill in a short form without leaving TikTok. TikTok's [help article](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/set-up-lead-generation-with-instant-form) walks through the setup: choose the Lead generation objective, select Instant Form as the optimisation location, then build or attach a form. Ask only for what you need (name, phone, one qualifying question).
- **Instant Messaging Ads.** These send people straight into a chat on WhatsApp or Messenger, optimised for clicks or conversations. TikTok's [setup guide](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/how-to-set-up-tiktok-instant-messaging-ads?lang=en) says WhatsApp needs "a unique phone number for the WhatsApp Business API" that is not used on any other WhatsApp account. It does not publish a country list, so confirm availability in your own Ads Manager.

### Paying for TikTok ads from Nigeria

This is where many Nigerian advertisers get stuck. Fintech guides from [Grey](https://grey.co/blog/pay-for-tiktok-ads-in-nigeria) and [EverTry](https://evertry.co/blog/running-tiktok-ads-from-nigeria-even-with-naira-card-issues/), both of which sell virtual dollar cards, report that TikTok bills in US dollars, that naira cards are often declined, and that most Nigerian advertisers pay with a virtual dollar card or a domiciliary account card. Both report minimum daily budgets of about $50 per campaign and $20 per ad group. Check the current minimums in Ads Manager before you plan a budget, and set a monthly cap you are comfortable losing while you test.

### A word on rules

The Advertising Regulatory Council of Nigeria (ARCON) has [warned](https://www.premiumtimesng.com/news/top-news/792469-arcon-bars-agencies-influencers-from-advertising-without-approval-warns-against-deceptive-promotions.html) that adverts, including on social media and through influencers, need approval before they are shown to the public. How far this reaches for small businesses' everyday posts is debated, and we are not lawyers. If you run paid campaigns, work with creators or make strong claims, check the current requirements with ARCON or a legal adviser. And if your Instant Form collects names and phone numbers, the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 applies: say what you will use the details for and only contact people about what they asked for.

## Step 6: Follow up every lead, automatically

TikTok leads are often colder than people who walked into your shop. They saw one video, tapped a form, and moved on to the next video. If nobody contacts them quickly, most will forget they ever asked.

A simple follow-up system looks like this:

1. **Get the lead out of TikTok immediately.** Instant Form leads can be downloaded from Ads Manager or synced to a CRM or Google Sheet through TikTok's integrations or a no-code tool.
2. **Alert the right person** on WhatsApp or email within minutes.
3. **Send a first message** that references what they asked about: "Hi Ada, thanks for asking about our bridal makeup packages on TikTok. Here is the price list..."
4. **Follow up two or three times** over the following days, then stop.
5. **Record the outcome** (quoted, booked, paid, lost) against the lead.

This is exactly the same problem as Facebook and Instagram leads, and our guide to [automating Facebook lead ads follow-up](/insights/automate-facebook-lead-ads-follow-up) explains the full set-up step by step. The tools change; the system does not.

## Step 7: Measure sales, not views

TikTok analytics will tell you views, watch time, followers and profile visits. Useful, but none of them pay rent. Every week, fill in a simple sheet with one row per video or campaign:

- Video or ad, date and type
- Views and profile visits (from TikTok analytics)
- WhatsApp chats or form leads (counted by keyword)
- Orders and naira value of sales
- Ad spend, if any

After a month you will know which kinds of video bring buyers and which only bring likes. Make more of the first kind. Paste the sheet into an AI assistant and ask it to spot patterns, then check its conclusions against what you saw. ## An illustrative example

*This is an illustrative walkthrough, not a client case study or a real result.*

A small skincare brand in Port Harcourt sells through Instagram and WhatsApp. The owner switches her TikTok to a Business Account, adds a wa.me link with the message "Hi, I saw you on TikTok", and pins a "how to order" video showing prices and delivery fees.

She commits to four videos a week across four types: how a product is made, answers to common customer questions, unboxing orders before dispatch, and customer reactions shared with permission. She uses an AI assistant to turn last week's customer questions into ten hooks, picks the four that sound like her and films them all on Saturday morning.

Every video ends with "Send GLOW on WhatsApp". Each Friday she counts GLOW chats and orders against each video in her sheet. After a month, two of her question-and-answer videos have clearly produced the most orders, so she promotes the better of the two with a small capped budget and keeps tracking orders, not views.

## Mistakes to avoid

**Using trending songs on a business account.** Stick to the Commercial Music Library or original audio.

**Posting without a next step.** Every video should tell people where to go and what to send.

**Leaving comments and chats unanswered.** TikTok creates conversations. Make sure someone owns them.

**Spending on ads before you have a winning video.** Test organically first, then put money behind what already works.

**Collecting leads you never contact.** A lead form without follow-up is wasted ad spend.

**Judging success by views.** A video with 2,000 views and six orders beats one with 200,000 views and none.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is TikTok good for small businesses in Nigeria?

For many consumer businesses, yes. TikTok reached an estimated 47.8 million adults in Nigeria in late 2025, according to DataReportal, and it rewards real, useful short videos. It works best when it is connected to a clear way to order, such as WhatsApp, and to a habit of tracking which videos produce sales.

### Should I use a personal or business account?

A Business Account gives you analytics, business tools and access to advertising. The main trade-off is music: business content must use the Commercial Music Library rather than the general library.

### Can I run TikTok ads with a naira card?

Sometimes, but often not. Nigerian fintech guides report that TikTok bills in US dollars and that naira cards are frequently declined, so most advertisers use a virtual dollar card or a domiciliary account card. Check current payment options in Ads Manager.

### Can TikTok ads send people to WhatsApp?

TikTok offers Instant Messaging Ads in Ads Manager and a messaging option in Promote that can send people to WhatsApp, but TikTok says some of these options are only available in select regions. Check whether they are offered on your account.

### Does TikTok pay Nigerian businesses or creators?

A Business Account is for marketing your own products and services, not for earning from views. TikTok's creator payment programmes are separate and their availability varies by country. For a business, the value of TikTok is the customers it brings in.

### Do I need to label AI-generated videos?

TikTok requires creators to label AI-generated content that contains realistic images, audio or video, and it may automatically label content made with its AI effects or carrying Content Credentials.
