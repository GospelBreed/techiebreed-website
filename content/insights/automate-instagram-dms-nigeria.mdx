Someone sees your post at 11:40pm and comments "How much?". Another person replies to your Story with "Available in size 12?". A third sends a DM asking if you deliver to Lekki. By the time you wake up and work through the pile, two of them have bought from someone who answered first.

For a lot of Nigerian businesses, Instagram is the shop window, the enquiry desk and the till all at once, and it runs on Direct Messages. That works when you get ten messages a day. It breaks when a post does well, when you run a promo, or when the one person who manages the page is busy, travelling or asleep.

Automating your Instagram DMs fixes the repetitive part of that work: the instant acknowledgement, the price and delivery answers, the link to pay, the reminder. This guide shows you how to do it properly, using Meta's free tools first and a dedicated tool when you outgrow them, within Meta's messaging rules and without turning your page into a cold robot. It is written for business owners and marketers who want to set this up themselves, and it is clear about where you will need technical help.

## Why Instagram DMs are worth automating

Instagram is a sizeable channel in Nigeria. The social media analytics firm NapoleonCat counted [about 10.4 million Instagram users in Nigeria in January 2026](https://stats.napoleoncat.com/instagram-users-in-nigeria/2026/01/), with people aged 25 to 34 the largest group. That is a younger, buying-age audience, and many of them prefer to ask before they pay.

Nigerian sellers have known this for years. When the Nigerian commerce platform Bumpa connected to Instagram's messaging API in 2022, the company told [Quartz](https://qz.com/nigeria-based-bumpa-is-adapting-instagram-messaging-for-1849358874) that vendors using Bumpa had received as much as 40% of their customer orders from Instagram and WhatsApp the year before. That figure is now dated and comes from one platform, but the pattern it describes, buying through chat, has not gone away.

The problem is trust and speed. In a January 2026 opinion piece for [Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2026/01/16/nigerias-social-media-economy-may-be-growing-faster-than-trust/), customer-experience practitioner Chidinma Asuni described how social media buying in Nigeria often fails: slow or vanishing replies, unclear delivery and refund terms, and buyers who quietly stop trusting sellers. Her suggested fix is simple: make expectations clear before payment. Good DM automation does exactly that, consistently, at any hour.

## What you can and can't automate on Instagram

Before you build anything, understand the rules. Meta sets them, and every tool, free or paid, has to follow them.

**You can reply automatically to people who message you.** When someone sends your professional account a message, Meta's [Messenger Platform documentation](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/messenger-platform/send-messages) gives you a 24-hour standard messaging window in which you may reply, and those replies may include promotional content.

**You can reply privately to people who comment.** Meta calls this a private reply. According to Meta's [Instagram Platform documentation on private replies](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/private-replies), you can send **one** message to someone who commented on your post, reel, story or ad post, **within 7 days** of the comment (for Instagram Live, only while the broadcast is running). The message lands in their main inbox if they follow you, or in their message requests if they don't, and it includes a link to the post they commented on. You can only send more messages if they reply, and then within 24 hours of their reply.

**A person can still reply later than 24 hours.** For conversations that need a human, Meta's [policy overview](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/messenger-platform/policy/policy-overview) describes a Human Agent tag that lets a business representative respond manually within 7 days. It is for people, not for automated promotions.

**You can't message people out of nowhere.** There is no Instagram equivalent of a bulk promotional broadcast to people who never messaged you. Meta's policy overview says sponsored messages and one-time notifications are not available for the Instagram messaging API.

**Automation must have a way out to a person.** Meta's [Instagram messaging overview](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/instagram-messaging/overview) says apps that use its Instagram messaging APIs must provide an escalation path to a human agent. That is good practice anyway: negotiations, complaints and custom orders belong with a person.

The practical takeaway: Instagram automation is about **responding**, quickly and well, to people who raised their hand. It is not a way to blast messages.

## Fix "DM for price" before you automate it

The most common Instagram automation in Nigeria is a bot that answers "How much?" in the DMs. Before you build that, ask a harder question: should people have to ask at all?

Section 115 of the Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Act 2018 deals with disclosure of the price of goods and services. The Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission summarises it on its [consumer rights page](https://fccpc.gov.ng/consumers/consumer-rights-responsibilities/rights-responsibilities/): businesses must give full disclosure of material facts, including prices, and the price displayed must match what the customer pays at checkout. The section treats a price as adequately displayed when it is written down, expressed in naira and attached to or published with the goods or services. Some Nigerian lawyers, including Abuja-based practitioner Stan Alieke in a [piece published by Tekedia](https://www.tekedia.com/it-is-illegal-for-internet-vendors-to-ask-interested-buyers-to-dm-for-price/), argue that this makes "DM for price" on social media an offence. We are not lawyers, and you should take proper legal advice for your situation, but the safe and customer-friendly position is clear: **show the price, or at least a "from" price, on the post itself.**

That changes what you automate. Instead of a bot that reveals a hidden price, you build automation that moves a buyer from interest to payment: availability, sizes, delivery fees and timelines, how to pay, and what happens if something goes wrong. The price is already on the post; the DM does the rest.

It also reduces wasted conversations. People who balk at the price leave before they message you, not after your team has typed a reply.

## Step 1: List the questions you answer every day

Open your Instagram inbox and scroll through the last two weeks of messages and comments. Write down every question you answered more than three times. For most product businesses, the list looks like this:

- Is it available? In which sizes or colours?
- How much is delivery to my area, and how long does it take?
- How do I pay? Do you accept pay on delivery?
- Where are you located? Can I come and pick it up?
- What is your return or exchange policy?
- Can I order in bulk or for an event?

Service businesses get versions of the same thing: price lists, availability, location, booking and turnaround times.

Now write one clear, friendly, current answer to each question. This is your reply library, and it matters more than any tool. If the answers are vague, out of date or inconsistent, automation simply delivers bad answers faster.

**Where AI helps:** paste your anonymised questions into an AI assistant and ask it to group them by theme and draft short answers in your brand voice, in plain English and, if your customers use it, Pidgin. Then edit every answer yourself. You are responsible for what goes out, especially prices, delivery promises and refund terms. The same questions make excellent post ideas, and our guide to [using AI to create social media content for a small business](/insights/ai-social-media-content-small-business-nigeria) shows how to turn them into a month of planned posts.

## Step 2: Switch on Meta's free automations

Start with what you already have. Meta's built-in tools cover a surprising amount for a small business, and they cost nothing.

**Instant reply.** In Meta Business Suite, under Inbox and then Automations, an instant reply greets people the first time they message you. Use it to set expectations: "Thanks for messaging [business]! Prices are on each post. Reply with the item name and your area and we'll confirm availability and delivery. We reply personally between 8am and 8pm."

**Away message.** Set it for the hours nobody is watching. Say when you will reply, and point to the answers people usually need so they can keep moving.

**Frequently asked questions.** In the Instagram app, you can show suggested questions when someone opens a chat with you. Instagram's [help page](https://help.instagram.com/ipad-app/4153704678000867) says you can add up to four, each with an automated answer. Use them for your top four questions from Step 1.

**Custom keywords.** Instagram's [Direct FAQ for businesses](https://business.instagram.com/direct-messaging/faq) explains that in Business Suite you can create a custom keyword automation with up to five keywords or phrases, choose when it sends, and add text, photos, videos and buttons. A message containing "delivery" can return your delivery fees and timelines; "pay" can return your payment options.

**Comment to message.** Meta's [Inbox automations help page](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/318238182723007) lists a comment-to-message template that sends a DM to people whose comments on your posts contain words you choose. It is set up on desktop. Test it on a post before you rely on it for a campaign, and remember the one-reply, 7-day limit described above.

Saved replies in the Instagram app are useful too: they are not automated, but they let whoever is answering send your approved answers with a shortcut instead of retyping them.

If you only do Steps 1 and 2, most small businesses will see faster first replies and far less repetitive typing. Many never need more. ## Step 3: Build comment-to-DM flows for posts and campaigns

Comment-to-DM is the automation behind posts that say "Comment SIZE and we'll send you the size guide" or "Comment MENU for our full menu with prices". Someone comments the keyword, gets an automatic DM, and the conversation starts in private, where you can actually sell.

To make it work:

1. **Pick one clear keyword per post.** "MENU", "SIZES", "BOOK". Tell people the keyword in the caption and on the image or video.
2. **Make the first DM complete.** Because the private reply is a single message until the person responds, put everything they asked for in it: the information, a link if relevant, and one easy question such as "Which size and area should we check for you?"
3. **Give them a reason to reply.** A reply opens the 24-hour window and lets your automation, or your team, continue.
4. **Reply publicly too, if your tool allows.** A short public reply ("Sent you a DM!") tells others the keyword works.
5. **Keep the post honest.** If the keyword unlocks a discount, say what it is and when it ends.

Meta's free comment-to-message template handles simple versions of this. When you want different flows per post, buttons that branch the conversation, follow-ups for people who went quiet, or data passed to a sheet or CRM, you will need a tool built on Meta's messaging API. Several are Meta Business Partners; check that any tool you choose uses Meta's official API, never asks for your Instagram password, and lets a person take over any conversation.

### A real example: LV Store

LV Store, a Brazilian women's fashion brand that sells mainly through Instagram, is a useful comparison because its problem will sound familiar to Nigerian fashion sellers. According to a [Manychat customer story](https://manychat.com/blog/success-stories/leticia-vaz/) published in March 2026, founder Letícia Vaz posted a daily "look of the day" Story with a 24-hour discount. Interest was high, but customers had to hunt for products on the website, and links were sent one by one by hand.

The brand automated those conversations so that people received the right product link as soon as they responded, then added automatic follow-ups for people who didn't click straight away. Manychat reports that on days when these campaigns ran, the store's website conversion rate rose from 1.3% to 3.5%.

This is a vendor-published result from a different market, so don't treat it as a forecast. The lesson that transfers is the diagnosis: **the demand was already there; the friction was in the reply.** Automation removed the wait between "I want this" and "here's where to pay".

## Step 4: Design the handover to a person

Automation should handle the predictable questions and then get out of the way. Decide in advance what always goes to a person:

- Negotiation, bulk orders and custom requests
- Complaints, wrong items, delays and refunds
- Anything your reply library doesn't cover
- Any customer who types "agent", "human" or "speak to someone"

Then decide who picks these up, during which hours, and how they are alerted. If a person has to respond after the 24-hour window, Meta's Human Agent tag allows manual replies within 7 days, so a weekend enquiry can still get a proper answer on Monday. If your automation tool has a shared inbox, assign conversations by label, such as "new order", "delivery issue" or "bulk", so nothing sits unclaimed.

If you use an AI assistant to reply in DMs, apply the same discipline we describe in our guide to [WhatsApp AI assistants for business](/insights/whatsapp-ai-assistant-for-business-nigeria): it should answer only from your approved information, say clearly when it cannot help, and pass the conversation to a person with the history attached.

## Step 5: Connect DMs to payment and delivery

A chat that ends in "I'll get back to you" isn't a sale. Close the loop:

**Send payment details at the moment of decision.** Your automation can send a payment link or account details as soon as someone confirms an item, size and address. Fewer messages between "I'll take it" and payment means fewer people changing their minds.

**State the terms before payment.** Delivery timeline, delivery fee, what happens if the item is out of stock, and your exchange or refund policy, in plain language. This is the "clear expectations before payment" fix the Nairametrics piece called for, and it protects you as much as the customer.

**Record the order somewhere other than the chat.** A shared sheet or order system with the customer's name, item, amount, address, payment status and source post. Once the order is confirmed, the follow-up usually moves to WhatsApp; our guide to [automating WhatsApp order confirmations and delivery updates](/insights/whatsapp-order-confirmation-automation-nigeria) covers that part.

**Ask before adding people to marketing lists.** Someone who asked about one dress has not agreed to receive your weekly promotions by email or WhatsApp. The Nigeria Data Protection Act expects a lawful basis, and for marketing that usually means consent; our [email marketing guide](/insights/email-marketing-small-business-nigeria) explains how to collect it properly.

## Step 6: Measure sales, not messages

Your automation tool will show you how many people triggered a flow. That's activity. What you need to know is which posts and keywords produced paying customers.

Give each campaign its own keyword or flow, and record the source on every order. At the end of each week, compare posts by orders and revenue, not by comments. You will often find that a post with modest engagement and a clear price outsells a viral post that attracted browsers. ## An illustrative example

*This is an illustrative walkthrough, not a client case study or a real result.*

A skincare seller in Ibadan posts three products a week and gets most enquiries through comments and DMs. Prices were previously "DM for price", and the owner answered every message by hand at night.

She adds the price and delivery fee to every post. She sets an instant reply and an away message in Meta Business Suite, four FAQs in the Instagram app (delivery, payment, location and authenticity), and keyword replies for "delivery" and "pay". For a new serum launch she asks people to comment "GLOW" for the full ingredient list and a launch discount; each commenter gets one DM with the list, the discount, the end date and the question "Which city should we deliver to?"

People who reply get delivery details and a payment link. Anyone asking about allergies, wholesale or a problem with an order is labelled for her to answer personally. Every order is logged in a sheet with the keyword that produced it. After a month, she can see which posts sell, which questions still come up, and which answers to add to her library next.

None of this needs custom software. It needs current answers, a few hours of setup and the habit of reviewing what happened.

## Mistakes to avoid

**Automating vague answers.** If your reply library says "price depends", automation spreads confusion.

**Hiding prices.** It wastes everyone's time and carries legal risk under the FCCPA.

**Using tools that ask for your password.** Unofficial bots that log in as you can get your account restricted. Use Meta's own tools or providers that connect through Meta's official API.

**Promising things automation can't do.** You cannot send unlimited follow-ups to commenters. One private reply, then only if they respond.

**No route to a person.** Customers who are stuck in a loop leave, and Meta requires an escalation path for apps on its API.

**Setting it and forgetting it.** Prices, stock and delivery fees change. Review your automated answers every time they do, and at least monthly.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I automate Instagram DMs for free?

Yes. Meta Business Suite includes instant replies, away messages, custom keyword replies and a comment-to-message template, and the Instagram app lets you set up to four frequently asked questions. These cover the basics for many small businesses. Paid tools add multi-step flows, follow-ups, integrations and reporting.

### Is it safe to use third-party Instagram automation tools?

Use tools that connect through Meta's official API and never ask for your Instagram password. Check what data the tool stores, who can see your conversations, and that a person can take over any chat.

### How many times can I message someone who commented on my post?

Once, automatically, within 7 days of their comment. If they reply, you can continue the conversation within 24 hours of their reply, under Meta's standard messaging window.

### Can AI answer my Instagram DMs?

Yes, as long as it answers only from information you have approved, admits when it doesn't know, and hands over to a person. Check its answers regularly, especially on prices, stock and delivery.

### Should I move Instagram customers to WhatsApp?

Often, yes, once they are ready to order, because delivery updates and payment reminders work well on WhatsApp. Ask first, and make it easy: a wa.me link with a pre-filled message tells you which post the customer came from.

### Do I need an Instagram business account?

You need a professional account (Business or Creator) for Meta's messaging tools, and an Instagram business account connected to Meta Business Suite to use its Inbox automations.
