A customer in Ikeja types "solar installer near me" into Google at 9pm. Three ads appear above the map. One leads to a slow website with no price. One leads to a phone number that rings out. The third opens a short page with a WhatsApp button, gets a reply within minutes and books a site survey for Saturday. All three paid roughly the same for that click. Only one got a customer.

That is the real lesson of Google Ads for a small business. Running the ads is the easy part. Getting paid in the right currency, showing up in the right places, tracking which clicks became sales and answering every enquiry fast is what decides whether the money comes back.

This guide shows you how to run Google Ads in Nigeria step by step: setting up an account you can actually pay for, building your first Search campaign, using Google's AI features without handing them your whole budget, tracking calls and WhatsApp chats, and automating the follow-up so no lead goes cold.

## Is Google Ads right for your business?

Google Ads works best when people already search for what you sell. Someone typing "cold room installation Lagos" or "accounting firm for small business Abuja" has a need and is comparing options now. Social media ads interrupt people; search ads answer them. That's why the two do different jobs, and why many businesses use both. (If you're running Meta ads to WhatsApp, our guide to [Click-to-WhatsApp ads in Nigeria](/insights/click-to-whatsapp-ads-nigeria) covers that side.)

Google Ads is a good fit when:

- customers search for your product or service by name, often with a location or a problem attached
- one new customer is worth enough to cover several clicks that don't buy
- someone can answer calls and WhatsApp messages quickly during the hours your ads run

It is a poor fit when nobody searches for what you sell yet (a brand-new kind of product), when your margin per sale is tiny, or when enquiries will sit unanswered. Fix the last one before spending anything.

Paid search also isn't a substitute for being findable for free. Ads stop the moment you stop paying; [SEO for a small business](/insights/seo-for-small-business-nigeria) and a well-kept [Google Business Profile](/insights/google-business-profile-nigeria) keep working. Ads are best used to win enquiries while those build, and to compete for the searches that matter most.

## Before you start: five things to have ready

1. **A destination that converts.** A dedicated page for the service you're advertising, with what's included, the areas you cover, pricing guidance, proof of real work and a clear WhatsApp button and phone number. Not your home page.
2. **Someone to answer.** Decide who replies to ad enquiries, on which number, and during which hours.
3. **A test budget you can afford to learn with.** Treat the first month as paid research.
4. **A definition of a good result.** A completed enquiry form? A call longer than a minute? A WhatsApp chat? A paid order? Write it down; you'll set Google up to count it.
5. **A simple place to record leads.** A CRM or even a shared sheet with columns for date, name, source, what they asked about, outcome and amount paid.

## Step 1: Set up an account you can pay for

This is where most Nigerian advertisers get stuck, so settle it before you build anything.

Go to ads.google.com, sign in with a business Google account (not a staff member's personal email) and, if the setup tries to rush you into a "smart" campaign, switch to the expert option so you can build a standard Search campaign yourself.

**The currency choice is permanent.** Google's help centre states plainly that you can't change the currency of an existing account; you'd have to open a new account and copy your campaigns across.

**Naira or dollars?** Google does run naira-billed accounts. Its help page on [operating charges for Nigerian Naira accounts](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13670594?hl=en) explains that since January 2024, naira accounts pay an extra "operating charge" on spend from ads shown to people outside Nigeria, at a rate Google publishes monthly (1% in every month listed for 2025 and 2026 at the time of writing). But self-serve naira billing has not been open to most small advertisers. When Mercurie launched AdPay, its managing director [told The Condia](https://thecondia.com/introducing-adpay-by-mercurie/) that unless you are a large advertiser with Google, you pay for Google Ads in dollars. In practice, small businesses use one of two routes:

- **A dollar account paid with a card that works for international charges**, such as a virtual dollar card from a licensed provider. Fintech guides such as Grey's note that many naira debit cards are declined on Google's recurring charges, so test your card with a small amount first.
- **A naira account opened through a Google Ads partner that handles naira billing.** AdPay by Mercurie and Dochase are examples that have publicly offered this; AdPay's own FAQ lists a 5.4% service fee on ad budget. We mention them as examples, not recommendations: compare fees, support and who owns the account before you sign up.

Whichever route you choose, budget for **7.5% VAT**, which Google has charged Nigerian advertisers [since April 2022](https://www.thecable.ng/from-april-1-google-to-deduct-7-5-vat-on-all-taxable-services-in-nigeria), and keep a funding routine so a declined card doesn't pause your ads mid-week.

## Step 2: Start with one Search campaign and one goal

When you create a campaign, choose **Leads** (or Sales, if people buy online) and the **Search** campaign type. Search shows text ads to people who type relevant searches, and it gives you the clearest view of what you're paying for: which searches triggered your ads, what they cost and what they produced.

Google will also offer **Performance Max**, which spreads your budget across Search, YouTube, Display, Maps and more and lets Google's AI decide where ads appear. It can work well for businesses with plenty of conversion data. For a first campaign with a modest budget, it gives you less direct control and less to learn from. Start with Search; add other types once you know what a lead costs you.

Keep the structure simple: one campaign for the area you actually serve, and inside it one ad group per service ("deep cleaning", "post-construction cleaning"), each pointing to its own page.

### What to do about AI Max

New Search campaigns now come with **AI Max** switched on by default, according to [Google's setup guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15909989?hl=en). It is an optimisation layer inside Search campaigns, not a separate campaign type. Its two main features, as Google [describes them](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15910187), are:

- **Search term matching**, which uses broad match and "keywordless" matching to show your ads on searches beyond your keyword list
- **Asset optimisation**, which uses generative AI to write extra headlines and descriptions from your ads and website, and can send people to the page on your site it predicts will work best

Used well, this finds customers you'd otherwise miss. Google's [launch post](https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/google-ai-max-for-search-campaigns/) reported that MyConnect, an Australia-based utility connection service, drove 16% more leads at a 13% lower cost per action after turning on AI Max, with a 30% increase in conversions from searches it wasn't previously reaching. That is a vendor-published result for an established advertiser, so read it as a possibility, not a forecast.

Used carelessly, it spends money on loosely related searches and shows ad text you didn't write. Google's [AI Max FAQ](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15913066) adds that search term matching doesn't work with manual CPC bidding and works best with conversion-based bidding. A sensible approach for a small account:

1. Get conversion tracking working first (Step 7).
2. Add your negative keywords and, if needed, exclude pages you never want ads to land on (your careers or blog pages, for example) using the URL controls.
3. Review the search terms and AI-written headlines every week, and remove anything inaccurate.

## Step 3: Show your ads only where and when you can serve

**Location.** Target the states, cities or radius you actually cover. Then open **Location options**. Google's default reaches people in your area *and* people who've shown interest in it, which can include someone in London searching for "Lekki apartment cleaning". That's useful for diaspora-facing businesses such as real estate. For a business that only serves people locally, choose **"Presence: People in or regularly in your targeted locations"**, the option described on Google's [advanced location options](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722038) page. On a naira account, this setting also helps you avoid the operating charge on spend outside Nigeria.

**Schedule.** If enquiries need a person to reply, run ads only when someone is available, or have an automatic first reply ready for after-hours chats (see Step 8).

**Budget.** You set an *average daily* budget. Google's [spending limits](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10486637?hl=en) page explains that a campaign can spend up to twice that amount on a busy day, but you won't be billed more than 30.4 times the daily budget in a month. To plan from a monthly figure, divide it by 30.4.

## Step 4: Choose keywords from your customers' own words

Your best keyword list is in your WhatsApp chats and call notes. Write down the exact phrases customers use before they buy: the service, the area, the problem ("generator not starting", "office cleaning Wuse 2 price"). Then:

- Group them by service, one group per ad group.
- Use **phrase match** ("office cleaning abuja") and **exact match** ([office cleaning abuja]) for your core terms to begin with, so you can see what works.
- Use Google's free **Keyword Planner** inside your account to find related searches and rough cost estimates. Treat those estimates as a range, not a promise.

**Negative keywords** stop wasted clicks. Before launch, add the obvious ones: *jobs, vacancy, salary, training, course, free, pdf, how to become* (unless you sell training). Then add more every week from your search terms report.

## Step 5: Write ads with AI help, and keep them honest

Google's main search ad format is the **responsive search ad**: you supply several headlines and descriptions and Google mixes them. According to [Google's guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7684791), each ad takes 3 to 15 headlines of up to 30 characters and 2 to 4 descriptions of up to 90 characters.

AI tools are genuinely useful here. Give one a short brief and ask for options:

> "I run [business] in [area]. Customers care about [speed, price clarity, guarantees]. We offer [specific services and real facts]. Write 15 Google Ads headlines under 30 characters and 4 descriptions under 90 characters. Use plain English, no exaggeration, and include the area in some headlines."

Then edit hard. Remove anything you can't prove ("No.1 in Lagos", "cheapest in Nigeria"), check every character count, and make sure the headlines still make sense in any order. Include at least one headline with the location and one with a clear, true next step, such as "Free Site Survey in Ibadan" if you really offer one.

**Add assets.** Call assets show your phone number; sitelinks point to other service pages; location assets connect your Business Profile. Google also has a **message asset** that opens a WhatsApp chat straight from the ad. Its [help page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14888522) says the feature is in beta and may not be available to every account yet, and that new advertisers' message asset impressions can be limited at first. If you see it, test it. If not, a WhatsApp button on your landing page does the same job.

**A regulatory note.** Nigeria's advertising regulator, ARCON, has directed that adverts, online ones included, must be vetted and approved before they're published. [Legit.ng](https://www.legit.ng/business-economy/technology/1698287-how-arcons-vetting-rules-squeezing-nigerian-businesses-influencers) describes the May 2025 directive and the complaints about cost and delays that followed. Enforcement against small online advertisers has been uneven, but the requirement exists, and regulated sectors (finance, health, property, education) should take it especially seriously. Check the current position with ARCON or a lawyer; this is general guidance, not legal advice.

## Step 6: Choose a bidding strategy that matches your data

Google's automated bidding learns from conversions. With no conversion data, it has nothing to learn from.

- **Weeks 1–4:** use **Maximise clicks** with a maximum cost-per-click limit, or manual CPC, so you control costs while tracking collects data.
- **Once you have steady conversions:** switch to **Maximise conversions**, then consider a target cost per acquisition once the numbers are stable.

One correction to a lot of older advice: **Enhanced CPC is gone** for Search and Display. Google's [ECPC page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2464964) confirms that campaigns still using it were moved to manual CPC from March 2025. Guides that tell you to "switch to Enhanced CPC after a month" are out of date.

## Step 7: Track the actions that matter

Without tracking, you can't tell a click that became a customer from one that left after two seconds. Set up conversions under **Goals** before you scale.

- **Calls from ads.** Google can count calls from your call asset as conversions once they last a minimum length you choose; the default is 60 seconds, according to [Google's call reporting guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9194359). Check in your account that call reporting works for your number and country; if it doesn't, use a dedicated phone line for ads and log those calls by hand.
- **WhatsApp clicks.** If you have the message asset, Google reports chats started from it. For the WhatsApp button on your landing page, set it up as a website conversion with the Google tag or Google Tag Manager, and give it its own pre-filled message ("Hi, I saw your ad about…") so the chat itself tells you the source.
- **Forms.** Count completed submissions on the thank-you page, not page visits.
- **Sales.** This is the one most small businesses skip. **Enhanced conversions for leads** lets you tell Google later which leads actually paid, matched using hashed contact details from your form. Google's [overview](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15713840?hl=en) explains the setup. When Google can see which leads became sales, its bidding starts finding more people like your buyers, not just more people who fill in forms.

Because this involves customer data, say in your privacy notice that you use advertising measurement tools, and keep only the data you need. The Nigeria Data Protection Act applies to these leads just as it does to your WhatsApp contact list.

Our guide to [measuring marketing ROI for a small business in Nigeria](/insights/measure-marketing-roi-small-business-nigeria) shows how to link each lead to a payment, even when customers pay by bank transfer, so you can work out cost per sale rather than cost per click.

## Step 8: Automate the follow-up so no lead goes cold

This is where digital marketing meets automation, and where most ad money is quietly lost. A paid lead that waits until tomorrow for a reply often buys from whoever answered first.

A simple follow-up system for Google Ads leads:

1. **Capture every lead in one place.** Form submissions, ad-sourced WhatsApp chats and calls all land in your CRM or sheet, tagged "Google Ads" and, ideally, with the campaign or keyword.
2. **Reply instantly.** An automatic first message acknowledges the enquiry, asks one qualifying question (location, size of job, timeline) and says when a person will follow up. A well-configured [WhatsApp AI assistant](/insights/whatsapp-ai-assistant-for-business-nigeria) can answer common questions out of hours and hand over to staff.
3. **Alert the right person.** A new lead triggers a notification to whoever covers that service or area, with the details already filled in.
4. **Follow up on a schedule.** If there's no reply, a reminder goes out after a day and the salesperson gets a task. Stop all messages the moment the customer replies or opts out.
5. **Record the outcome.** Won, lost or no response, plus the amount paid. That outcome is what you send back to Google through enhanced conversions for leads.

The same pattern works for lead forms on other platforms. Our step-by-step guide to [automating Facebook lead ads follow-up](/insights/automate-facebook-lead-ads-follow-up) builds it end to end, and the tools carry over directly to Google Ads leads.

## Step 9: Run a 30-minute weekly review

Google Ads rewards steady attention more than clever tricks. Once a week:

- **Search terms report:** add irrelevant searches as negatives and good ones as keywords.
- **Ads:** pause headlines that are inaccurate or never show, and check any AI-written text.
- **Leads:** were they all answered, and how fast? Missed calls are a staffing problem, not an advertising one.
- **Cost per lead and cost per sale** by ad group: move budget toward what produces paying customers.
- **Billing:** is your card funded for the next charge?

Change one or two things at a time and give each change a week or two before judging it.

## An illustrative example: a solar installer in Ibadan

*This is an illustrative example, not a TechieBreed client.*

A four-person solar installation company in Ibadan gets most of its work from referrals and wants more enquiries for home inverter and solar systems.

- **Account:** opened in dollars and paid with a virtual dollar card, after confirming the card works for recurring international charges.
- **Campaign:** one Search campaign targeting Ibadan and nearby towns, set to "Presence" only, running 7am to 8pm when the sales lead can answer.
- **Ad groups:** "home solar installation", "inverter installation" and "solar battery replacement", each with its own page showing real installations, what each package includes and a WhatsApp button with a pre-filled message.
- **Tracking:** WhatsApp button clicks and calls over 60 seconds counted as conversions; every enquiry logged in a shared sheet with outcome and amount paid.
- **Follow-up:** an instant WhatsApp reply asks for the property type and current power needs, and the sales lead gets an alert. Unanswered leads get one reminder the next day.
- **After four weeks:** the owner reviews cost per site survey and cost per installation by ad group, cuts the weakest, adds negatives ("solar panel price list pdf", "solar installer jobs") and switches to Maximise conversions.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Picking a currency without checking how you'll pay.** Confirm your payment route first; the currency can't be changed later.
- **Sending ads to your home page.** One service, one page, one clear next step.
- **Leaving location on the default** when you only serve one city.
- **Switching on every AI feature on day one** with no conversion tracking and no review routine.
- **Counting clicks instead of customers.** A cheap click that never buys is expensive.
- **Running ads nobody answers.** Every unanswered lead is money spent for a competitor.
- **Following outdated guides** that still recommend Enhanced CPC or "set and forget".

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I pay for Google Ads in naira?

Naira-billed accounts exist, but self-serve naira billing generally hasn't been available to small advertisers. Most small businesses either open a dollar account and pay with a card that supports international recurring charges, or open a naira account through a Google Ads partner that handles naira billing for a fee. Decide before you create the account, because Google doesn't let you change an account's currency.

### How much does Google Ads cost in Nigeria?

There's no fixed price. You pay per click in an auction, and the cost depends on your industry, competition, location and how relevant your ads and pages are. Use Keyword Planner in your account for estimates, set an average daily budget you're comfortable with (Google won't bill more than 30.4 times it in a month), and judge success by cost per sale, not cost per click. Add 7.5% VAT.

### Do I need a website to run Google Ads?

You need somewhere for the click to go, and a focused landing page usually converts far better than a home page or social profile. It doesn't need to be a big site: one clear page per service, fast on mobile, with a WhatsApp button and a phone number someone answers, is enough to start.

### Should I use AI Max and Performance Max?

They can work well once Google has conversion data to learn from. For a new, small account, start with a standard Search campaign, get tracking right, then use AI Max with negative keywords, URL controls and a weekly review of search terms and AI-written ads. Consider Performance Max once you know your cost per lead and have steady conversions.

### Do my Google ads need ARCON approval?

ARCON has directed that adverts, including online ones, need its approval before publication, and has warned of sanctions for unapproved ads. How strictly that is enforced against small online advertisers is a separate question. Check the current requirements with ARCON or a lawyer, especially in regulated sectors.

### Is Google Ads better than Facebook ads for a small business?

They do different jobs. Google Ads reaches people actively searching for what you sell; Facebook and Instagram ads reach people based on interests and behaviour, often before they're searching. Many businesses use Google for high-intent searches and Meta for awareness and retargeting, and compare cost per sale across both.
