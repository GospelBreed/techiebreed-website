You run a lead ad on Facebook or Instagram. The form is short, the cost per lead looks good, and by Friday Ads Manager says you have 140 new leads. Then someone on your team downloads the spreadsheet, starts calling, and hears the same things again and again: "Who is this?", "I don't remember filling any form", or the phone simply rings out.

Most of the time the ad did its job. What failed is the follow-up. Leads sat inside Meta for days, nobody was alerted, the first contact came too late, and there was no record of who had been called, who replied and who paid.

This guide shows you how to automate Facebook lead ads follow-up properly: how to get each lead out of Meta the moment it arrives, alert the right person, send a first message within minutes, follow up without spamming, and measure which ads produce paying customers. It is written for business owners, marketers and operations managers, with examples from Nigeria, and it is honest about which parts you can build yourself with no-code tools and which need a technical hand.

## Why lead form leads go cold so quickly

A lead form ad (Meta calls the form an **Instant Form**) is built to be easy. The form opens inside Facebook or Instagram, often pre-fills the person's name, email and phone number, and takes a few seconds to submit. That ease is the reason the cost per lead is low, and also the reason the leads are fragile.

Compare it with someone who sends you a WhatsApp message from a Click-to-WhatsApp ad. That person has started a conversation and is waiting for a reply. A form lead has done something quicker and less committed. They may have tapped through between other posts and forgotten your business name an hour later. If you are choosing between the two formats, our guide to [Click-to-WhatsApp ads in Nigeria](/insights/click-to-whatsapp-ads-nigeria) covers the chat-first route; this article is about making forms work.

Speed matters for any online enquiry. In their Harvard Business Review article [The Short Life of Online Sales Leads](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads), Oldroyd, McElheran and Elkington concluded that "most companies are not responding nearly fast enough." With form leads the problem is worse, because nothing in Meta tells you a lead has arrived unless you set it up. By default, leads simply collect in Meta, waiting to be downloaded.

The common failure points look like this:

- **Leads stay in Meta.** Someone downloads a CSV once or twice a week, so the first call comes days after the form.
- **No one owns new leads.** The file goes into a group chat and three people call the same person, or nobody does.
- **No first message.** The lead hears nothing until a stranger calls, so they don't connect the call to the ad.
- **No follow-up sequence.** One unanswered call and the lead is forgotten.
- **No outcome recorded.** Ads Manager reports cost per lead, but nobody knows which ad produced a sale.

Every one of these is a process problem, and every one can be automated.

## The follow-up system in one picture

Before choosing tools, agree on the flow. A good lead ads follow-up system has six steps:

1. **Capture.** The lead leaves Meta automatically within minutes of submitting the form.
2. **Store.** It lands in one place your team works from: a shared sheet to start, a CRM as you grow.
3. **Alert and assign.** The right person is notified straight away, with the lead's details and the ad it came from.
4. **First contact.** The lead receives an immediate message (email, SMS or WhatsApp) that names your business and what they asked about, followed by a call from a person.
5. **Follow up.** A short, timed sequence continues until the lead replies, books, buys or says no.
6. **Record and learn.** Every lead ends with an outcome, so you can see your real cost per qualified lead and per sale, by ad.

The rest of this guide walks through each step.

## Step 1: Build the form so follow-up is easier

Good follow-up starts in the form itself. When you create the Instant Form, think about what your team needs in order to act.

**Ask one or two qualifying questions.** A property developer might ask for budget range and preferred location; a training school might ask which course and start month; a clinic might ask which service and preferred day. Multiple-choice answers are easier to sort automatically than free text. Don't add so many questions that nobody finishes the form.

**Consider the higher-intent form type.** Meta offers a form type designed for higher intent, which adds a review step before the person submits. You will usually get fewer leads, but more of them will remember filling it in. Test it against the standard form rather than assuming.

**Say what happens next.** The form's intro and completion screen are your first chance to set expectations: "We'll send you the price list on WhatsApp and call you within the hour." People answer calls they are expecting.

**Be clear about consent.** Meta requires a link to your privacy policy on lead forms. Beyond that, say plainly how you will contact people. Under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023, people have the right to object to direct marketing, and on WhatsApp the [Business Messaging Policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/) requires opt-in before a business messages someone. Filling in a form for one product is not permission for weekly promotions.

## Step 2: Get leads out of Meta automatically

This is the step that makes everything else possible. You have three realistic options.

### Option A: Meta's built-in CRM integrations

Meta can send leads directly to a number of CRMs and email tools through its own integrations. If you already use one of the supported CRMs, start there: it is usually the simplest and most stable route. Check what your CRM supports before buying anything new.

### Option B: A no-code automation tool (Zapier, Make or n8n)

For most small businesses, a no-code automation tool is the most flexible route, because it can send each lead to several places at once: a Google Sheet, an email to the sales team, a WhatsApp or SMS message and a CRM.

- **Zapier** has a Facebook Lead Ads "New Lead" trigger, which its [help page](https://zapier.com/apps/facebook-lead-ads/help) describes as starting a Zap instantly. You select the Page and the specific form to watch. At the time of writing, Zapier lists Facebook Lead Ads as a premium app that needs a paid plan, so check current pricing.
- **Make** has a Facebook Lead Ads app with a **Watch Leads** module and a **Get Lead Details** module. Its [documentation](https://apps.make.com/facebook-lead-ads) notes that a form must have been submitted at least once before it will load in the Get Lead Details module, so send a test lead first.
- **n8n** has a [Facebook Lead Ads Trigger](https://docs.n8n.io/integrations/builtin/trigger-nodes/n8n-nodes-base.facebookleadadstrigger) node with a "New lead" event. It uses your own Meta app, and n8n's [credentials guide](https://docs.n8n.io/integrations/builtin/credentials/facebookleadads) explains that the app must be taken live, which includes a privacy policy URL and business verification. n8n also warns that Meta allows only one webhook per app, so switching between test and production URLs overwrites the registered webhook. This option suits teams that want to self-host or need more control, and it takes more setup.

### Option C: A direct integration with Meta's API

Your developer can subscribe your Page to lead webhooks and fetch each lead from Meta's Graph API. Meta's [guide to retrieving leads](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/ads-commerce/marketing-api/guides/lead-ads/retrieving) explains that the webhook tells you a lead exists (its ID, the form and the ad) and your system then requests the lead's answers. Meta says real-time notifications can arrive "with a delay of up to a few minutes," which is still far better than a weekly download. This route makes sense when lead volume is high or leads must flow into custom software.

### The permission that trips everyone up: Leads Access

Whichever tool you choose, Meta must allow it to read your leads. In Meta Business settings, under **Integrations > Leads Access**, you can control which people and which CRMs can access leads for each Page. Make's documentation, for example, tells you to go to CRMs, choose Assign CRMs and select Make. Meta's developer guide notes that if nobody has customised Leads Access, all Page admins have access; once a business admin customises it, access depends on that configuration. If your automation connects but no leads arrive, check this setting first.

**Test before you spend.** Meta provides a lead ads testing tool for submitting test leads. Send a few through and confirm each one lands in your sheet or CRM with the right fields, the right form name and the right ad name.

### Don't treat Meta as your database

Even with automation running, leads stored in Meta do not stay downloadable forever; Meta's Help Centre has a page on expired leads. Your sheet or CRM should be the record you trust, and Meta should be where leads come from, not where they live.

## Step 3: Alert the right person and assign the lead

A lead that arrives silently in a spreadsheet is only slightly better than one sitting in Meta. Set up an alert for every new lead:

- **Who:** the person or team who owns that form, product or location. If you run separate forms for Lagos and Abuja, route each to the right team.
- **Where:** wherever your team actually looks. For many Nigerian teams that is WhatsApp; for others it is email, Slack or the CRM's mobile app.
- **What:** name, phone number, answers to the qualifying questions, the form and ad name, and a link to the lead's record.

Then assign ownership. A simple round-robin (lead one to Tolu, lead two to Chidi) avoids double-calling. Most CRMs and automation tools can do this automatically. Add a rule for out-of-hours leads: either an on-call person, or a clear first message that says when you will call.

## Step 4: Send the first message within minutes

The first message has one job: help the lead connect your business to the form they just submitted, so they pick up when you call.

**By email.** Easy to automate from any tool. Use your business name in the sender name and subject line, repeat what they asked about, and include the one thing they most want (a price list, a brochure or available dates).

**By SMS.** Short and widely read, useful when email open rates are low. Keep it plain: who you are, what they asked about, and when you'll call.

**By WhatsApp.** Often the most effective channel in Nigeria, but it has rules. A form lead has not messaged you, so your first WhatsApp message is business-initiated. On the WhatsApp Business Platform that means using a pre-approved message template and having the person's opt-in, and template messages are charged per delivered message by category and country. Our guide to [WhatsApp AI assistants](/insights/whatsapp-ai-assistant-for-business-nigeria) explains the Business app versus the Platform. A practical alternative is to invite the lead to message you first: include a click-to-chat link in the completion screen and the confirmation email, so the conversation starts on their side.

Then the call. Automation does not replace the person; it makes sure the person calls quickly and knows who they are calling.

## Step 5: Follow up with a short, timed sequence

Most form leads will not answer the first call. Plan the next steps in advance so nobody has to remember them:

- **Day 0:** instant message, then a call within the hour during working hours.
- **Day 1:** if there was no answer, a second call at a different time of day, plus a short message with something useful, such as an answer to a common question.
- **Day 3:** a final message that makes replying easy: "Reply 1 if you'd like the price list, 2 if now isn't a good time."
- **Then stop,** or move them to a slower, consented newsletter.

The automation should **stop the sequence as soon as the lead responds**, books or buys, or opts out. That is the difference between follow-up and spam. If you use a CRM, the stop happens when someone changes the lead's stage. If you use a sheet, a status column ("Contacted", "Qualified", "Not interested", "Won") can drive the same logic.

Our guide to [automating payment reminders](/insights/automate-payment-reminders-nigeria) uses the same principle further down the funnel: send reminders on a schedule and stop them the moment the event happens.

## Step 6: Record outcomes and measure cost per sale

Ads Manager shows cost per lead. What you need is cost per **qualified** lead and cost per **sale**, by ad and by form. That only works if every lead ends with an outcome.

### The manual method

Add three columns to your lead sheet or CRM: qualified (yes or no), sold (yes or no) and amount. Because the automation stores the ad and form name with each lead, at the end of each week you can divide each ad's spend by its qualified leads and its sales. Within a few weeks you usually find that the cheapest leads are not the most profitable ones.

### The connected method: Conversions API for CRM

Meta can also learn from your outcomes. With **Conversions API for CRM**, your CRM sends lead stages, such as qualified or converted, back to Meta, and you can run campaigns with the **Conversion Leads** performance goal so Meta looks for people more likely to become customers, not just to fill in forms. Meta's [developer documentation](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/conversions-api/conversion-leads-integration) says this goal is currently only compatible with lead ads (Instant Forms), recommends storing the 15–17 digit Meta Lead ID in your CRM, and lists fit guidelines including at least 200 leads per month, uploading data at least once a day, and a target stage that happens within 28 days of the lead.

If you are below those volumes, the manual method is the right place to start. If you are above them, this is worth doing. Make, for example, [announced](https://www.make.com/blog/meta-make-conversions-api-for-crm) a Conversions API for CRM app that it says is free of charge for two years from February 2026, built as one scenario for capturing leads and another for sending status updates to Meta.

## A real example of the pipeline side

The capture step gets leads into your system. What happens next depends on having a proper pipeline. TechieBreed's [Roseway CRM case study](/our-work/roseway-crm) shows what that looks like for a property services business: every prospective partner enters as a lead, every outreach touch across email, calls, LinkedIn and meetings is logged, deals move across a board from new lead to contacted, qualified, discovery call, proposal and pilot term, and a single task list shows what follow-up is due today. That project was about partners rather than ad leads, and its results are still being measured, but the structure of leads, logged touches, stages and a daily task list is exactly what a lead ads follow-up system needs.

## An illustrative example

*This is an illustrative walkthrough, not a client case study or a real result.*

A vocational training school in Abuja runs an Instant Form ad for a weekend data analysis course. The form asks for name, phone, email and one question: "When would you like to start?" (next month, in two to three months, just looking).

A Make scenario watches the form. Each new lead is added to a Google Sheet with the ad name, emailed to the admissions officer, and sent an email with the course outline and fees. Leads who chose "next month" are flagged as priority, and the admissions officer calls them the same day. Leads who chose "just looking" get the email and one follow-up a week later, then nothing more unless they reply.

After a month, the school reads its sheet rather than Ads Manager. One ad brought cheaper leads, but almost all of them chose "just looking". The other cost more per lead but produced most of the enrolments. Budget moves accordingly. Nothing here needed custom code. It needed a clear flow, one place to record outcomes and an automation that ran every time.

## Common mistakes to avoid

**Downloading leads by hand.** Weekly CSV downloads guarantee slow follow-up. Automate capture first.

**Forgetting Leads Access.** Most "my automation isn't working" problems come down to the CRM or tool not being given access to the Page's leads.

**Sending everyone the same sequence.** Use the qualifying answer to separate ready-now leads from browsers.

**Messaging on WhatsApp without opt-in or templates.** It risks blocks and restrictions on your number and breaks the trust you just paid for.

**Never stopping.** A sequence that keeps messaging someone who already said no damages your brand.

**Judging ads by cost per lead.** A cheap lead that never answers is expensive.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I automatically send Facebook leads to Google Sheets?

Yes. Zapier, Make and n8n can all add each new lead to a Google Sheet as it arrives. Some CRMs also integrate with Meta directly. A sheet is a good starting point; move to a CRM when several people are working the leads or you need stages and tasks.

### Why is my Facebook lead ads integration not receiving leads?

Check four things: the tool has been given access under Leads Access in your Meta business settings; the person who connected it is an admin with the right permissions on the Page; the automation is watching the correct Page and form; and you have sent a test lead with Meta's testing tool. With n8n, also check that your Meta app is live and that the production webhook hasn't been overwritten by a test URL.

### Can I send an automatic WhatsApp message to Facebook lead ad leads?

Yes, through the WhatsApp Business Platform using an approved message template, and only to people who have opted in to WhatsApp messages from you. Template messages are charged by Meta. If you would rather avoid that, invite leads to message you first with a click-to-chat link in your completion screen or confirmation email.

### How quickly should I follow up a Facebook lead?

As fast as you can, ideally within minutes for the automatic message and within the hour for the call during working hours. Research on online leads, such as the Harvard Business Review study cited above, consistently finds that slow response loses leads.

### Do I need a CRM for Facebook lead ads?

Not to start. A well-kept shared sheet with automated capture works for low volumes. You need a CRM when several people share leads, when you need automatic assignment and follow-up tasks, or when you want to send lead stages back to Meta with Conversions API for CRM.

### Are Facebook lead ads better than Click-to-WhatsApp ads?

Neither is better in general. Forms collect details at a lower cost per lead but need fast, organised follow-up. Click-to-WhatsApp ads start a conversation straight away but need someone, or something, to reply instantly. Many businesses test both and compare cost per sale, not cost per lead.
