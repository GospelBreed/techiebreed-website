If your business sells or serves through WhatsApp, you already know the pattern. A customer asks "How much?" at 10:47pm. Another sends a voice note asking whether you deliver to Lekki. Someone else wants to know if the Saturday class still has space. By the time a member of staff gets to these messages the next morning, at least one of those customers has bought from whoever replied first.

A WhatsApp AI assistant is one of the most practical ways to close that gap. Done well, it answers the routine questions instantly, collects the details your team needs, and passes the conversations that matter to a person, with the context already attached. Done badly, it frustrates customers, gives wrong prices and puts your number at risk.

This guide is written for Nigerian business owners and operations leads who want the first outcome. It covers what an assistant should and shouldn't handle, what changed in Meta's rules in 2026, what it actually costs to run, how to set one up step by step, and the mistakes that most often trip businesses up when they rush in.

## Why WhatsApp is where the work piles up

For many Nigerian businesses, WhatsApp is not just one channel among several. It is the shopfront, the customer service desk and the sales pipeline at once. The scale is hard to ignore: the Nigeria Smartphone Study 2025 by Orange Group and KPMG, [reported by Brand Spur](https://brandspurng.com/2026/09/26/whatsapp-installed-on-95-of-nigerian-smartphones-study-finds/), found WhatsApp installed on 95% of the smartphones it surveyed across 12 major cities. That is an installation figure, not a count of active users, but it tells you where your customers already are.

The problem is that WhatsApp was designed for conversations, not operations. Enquiries sit in one person's phone. There is no shared record of who asked what, which leads were followed up, or how long customers waited. When that person is busy, on leave or asleep, the business effectively stops responding. If that sounds familiar, it is one of the clearest [signs your business is ready for automation](/insights/signs-your-business-is-ready-for-automation).

## What a WhatsApp AI assistant should handle (and what it shouldn't)

The most useful way to think about an assistant is as a well-briefed front desk, not a replacement for your team. It should take on the repetitive first layer of every conversation so your people can spend their time on the conversations that need judgement.

### Good jobs for an assistant

An assistant earns its keep on questions that have a correct, consistent answer your business has already decided. That includes prices and packages, opening hours, locations and delivery areas, product availability, payment options, and the status of an order or booking that already exists in one of your systems. It is also good at **collecting information**: a customer's name, what they want, their budget, preferred date or location, so that when a person picks up the conversation they are not starting from "Hello, how may I help you?"

Beyond answering, an assistant can take simple actions. It can book an inspection or consultation slot, send a payment link, confirm that a payment has arrived, or send a reminder the day before an appointment, provided it is connected to the systems that hold that information.

### Jobs that should stay with people

Anything involving negotiation, complaints, refunds, credit decisions, sensitive personal circumstances or a customer who is clearly upset should go to a person quickly. So should anything your business has not documented. If your team cannot write down the answer, the assistant should not be inventing one.

A simple test: if a new staff member in their first week could answer the question correctly using your price list and FAQ, the assistant can probably handle it. If they would need to ask a manager, the assistant should hand over.

## What Meta's 2026 rules mean for your business

There was a lot of confusion when Meta updated its WhatsApp Business Solution terms in late 2025. Headlines said WhatsApp was "banning AI chatbots", and some business owners understandably assumed that meant they could no longer use AI on WhatsApp at all. That is not what happened.

The change, which [TechCrunch reported in October 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/18/whatssapp-changes-its-terms-to-bar-general-purpose-chatbots-from-its-platform/), took effect on 15 January 2026. It bars "AI Providers" from using the WhatsApp Business Solution to distribute general-purpose assistants, the kind of open-ended chatbot that answers any question about anything. Meta told TechCrunch the change doesn't affect businesses that are using AI to serve their own customers on WhatsApp.

In practical terms, an assistant that answers questions about *your* products, takes *your* bookings and handles *your* customer enquiries is the intended use. An assistant that lets people chat about anything at all through your number is not. That is a good design principle anyway: a focused assistant is more accurate and easier to trust.

The rule that matters more day to day is older. WhatsApp's [Business Messaging Policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/) allows automation when you respond within the 24-hour customer service window, but says you must also have "prompt, clear, and direct escalation paths", such as transfer to a human agent. The same policy requires opt-in before you message people and asks businesses to give clear instructions for opting out. Build those requirements in from day one rather than bolting them on later.

## WhatsApp Business app or WhatsApp Business Platform?

This is the first real decision, and it shapes everything else.

The free **WhatsApp Business app** gives you a business profile, a catalogue, labels, quick replies, and simple greeting and away messages. For a small business with one or two people handling chats, those built-in tools are worth setting up properly before you spend money on anything else. What the app cannot do is connect to your other systems, route conversations between team members in a structured way, or run an AI assistant that answers from your own information.

The **WhatsApp Business Platform** (often called the API or Cloud API) is what assistants and integrations are built on. It lets your number connect to a CRM, a booking system, a payment provider or an AI model, and lets several staff work the same inbox. You usually access it through a Meta Business Solution Provider or a developer who builds on Meta's Cloud API directly.

There is now a middle path. Meta's "coexistence" onboarding, described in its [developer documentation](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/embedded-signup/onboarding-business-app-users), lets a business connect its existing WhatsApp Business app number to the Cloud API and keep using the app for one-to-one chats. Messages are mirrored between the two, and chat history from up to 180 days before onboarding can be shared, with the business's approval. There are trade-offs worth knowing before you switch: according to Meta's documentation, app tools such as quick replies, labels and away messages are not supported on the Cloud API side, group chats are not synchronised, existing broadcast lists become read-only and linked companion devices are unlinked when you onboard (you can re-link supported ones). For a business that does not want to lose its number or its history, coexistence is often still the right route, but go in with your eyes open.

## What it costs to run

There are two separate costs to plan for: what Meta charges for messages, and what it costs to build and run the assistant itself.

Since 1 July 2025, Meta has charged [per message rather than per conversation](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing/) on the Business Platform, and it updated its pricing again on 1 October 2026. The parts that matter most for an enquiry assistant:

- **Replies to customers now have a monthly free allowance.** When a customer messages you, a 24-hour customer service window opens, and only then can you send non-template replies. Since 1 October 2026, Meta gives each business phone number 1,000 free service messages a month and charges for service replies after that. Replies generated by Meta's own Business Agent are charged separately, per token.
- **Click-to-WhatsApp ads can open a free entry point window.** If a customer messages you from a Click-to-WhatsApp ad on the mobile app and you reply within 24 hours, Meta's pricing documentation (updated 30 September 2026) says a free window opens for up to 7 days, during which marketing, utility, authentication and service messages are not charged. Our guide to [running Click-to-WhatsApp ads in Nigeria](/insights/click-to-whatsapp-ads-nigeria) explains how to use that window for follow-up.
- **Messages you start are charged.** Marketing, utility and authentication templates sent outside free windows are billed per message at rates that vary by country. Nigeria has its own rate in Meta's rate card, so check the current figures before you plan a broadcast campaign.

The build and running cost is where quotes vary widely. You will typically pay for a messaging platform or hosting, the AI model's usage, and the setup work: writing the knowledge base, connecting your systems, testing and training your team. A simple FAQ-and-handover assistant is a much smaller project than one that checks stock, takes payments and updates a CRM. Ask any provider to separate these costs so you can see what you are paying for.

## How to set up a WhatsApp AI assistant, step by step

The businesses that get good results tend to spend more time on the first three steps than on the technology. The tool is the easy part. Knowing exactly what the assistant should do is the hard part.

### 1. Map the conversations you already have

Scroll back through a few weeks of chats and sort them. What are the ten questions you answer most often? Which enquiries turn into sales, and what information did your team need to close them? Where do customers wait the longest? You will often find that a handful of question types make up most of the volume. Those are your starting point.

### 2. Write the answers down

An AI assistant is only as accurate as the information you give it. Build a short, structured knowledge base: current prices, packages, delivery areas and fees, opening hours, payment methods, policies on refunds and rescheduling, and the exact wording you want for sensitive answers. Decide who owns this document and how often it gets updated, because a price change that never reaches the assistant is how businesses end up quoting last quarter's rates.

### 3. Define the handover rules

Decide in advance when the assistant should stop and bring in a person. Typical triggers include a customer asking for a human, a complaint or refund request, an order above a certain value, a question the knowledge base does not cover, or the same question being asked twice without resolution. Decide who receives each type of handover, during which hours, and what the customer is told while they wait. This is also how you meet WhatsApp's escalation requirement.

### 4. Choose your route and connect your systems

Pick between the Business app with its built-in tools, a Business Platform setup through a provider, or coexistence if you want to keep your current number and history. Then decide which systems the assistant needs to read from or write to. For most businesses that means at least a place to log leads, such as a CRM or a well-structured spreadsheet, and often a calendar, a payment provider such as Paystack or Flutterwave, or an order system. This is the part that turns an auto-responder into an operational tool, and it is where [business systems integration](/what-we-do#integrations) matters.

### 5. Get consent and data handling right

Collect opt-in properly before you send anyone template messages, and make it easy to opt out. Be clear in your privacy notice that customers may be speaking with an automated assistant, what information you collect and where it is stored. The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 gives people the right to object to their data being used for direct marketing, and restricts decisions based solely on automated processing where they have legal or similarly significant effects on the person. In practice: keep consequential decisions, such as approving credit or rejecting an application, with a person. If your business handles sensitive data, get proper legal advice on your obligations.

### 6. Test with real questions before launch

Run the assistant against the real questions you collected in step one, including awkward ones: Pidgin, misspellings, voice notes, questions about things you do not sell, attempts to get a discount. Have your team try to break it. Fix the knowledge base and handover rules based on what you find. Launch to a small group or to out-of-hours enquiries first, rather than switching everything over at once.

### 7. Measure, review and improve

Agree what success looks like before launch. Useful measures include time to first response, the share of enquiries resolved without a handover, how many handed-over leads convert, and how often customers ask for a person. Review a sample of conversations every week for the first month. The best improvements usually come from reading transcripts, not from changing the technology.

## What this looks like in practice

Here are three illustrative scenarios, simplified from the kinds of workflows businesses commonly ask about. They are examples of how a setup could work, not reports of specific client results.

**A property company.** Prospective tenants message about available units. The assistant answers questions about location, rent, service charge and availability from an up-to-date listings sheet, asks for the enquirer's budget, move-in date and preferred area, and offers inspection slots from the agent's calendar. Confirmed inspections land in the CRM with the conversation attached, and the agent gets a reminder the morning of the visit. Existing residents reporting a fault are routed to a separate flow that logs the request and gives them a reference number. You can see how we approach this kind of work for [real estate and property businesses](/industries#property), and our guide to [real estate lead follow-up automation](/insights/real-estate-lead-follow-up-automation-nigeria) covers the wider sales process from first enquiry to inspection and payment plan.

**A training provider.** Prospective students ask about course dates, fees and format. The assistant answers, sends a payment link, and once payment is confirmed, triggers enrollment and sends joining instructions. Anyone asking about instalment plans or corporate group bookings is handed to the admissions lead. This mirrors the workflows described on our [education and EdTech](/industries#education) page.

**A professional services firm.** Enquiries arrive through WhatsApp, the website and Instagram. The assistant qualifies each one with three or four questions, books a discovery call for promising leads and logs everything in one pipeline, so the partners can see every enquiry in one place instead of across three phones.

## Common mistakes to avoid

**Launching without a handover path.** A customer who cannot reach a person when they need one is more likely to leave than one who simply waited for a reply. It also runs against WhatsApp's own policy.

**Letting the assistant guess.** Assistants should be instructed to answer only from your approved information and to hand over when they do not know. A confident wrong answer about price or availability costs more than a short wait.

**Treating it as a broadcast tool.** Sending unsolicited promotional messages to everyone in your contacts is the fastest way to get your number reported and restricted. Use templates for customers who have opted in, and keep them relevant.

**Forgetting the knowledge base after launch.** Prices, stock and policies change. Without an owner and a routine for updates, accuracy drifts within weeks.

**Automating a broken process.** If your team does not agree on how a lead should be qualified or what happens after a booking, automation will simply do the confused thing faster. Fix the process first. This is why we recommend starting with an [AI Operations Audit](/insights/what-happens-in-an-ai-operations-audit) rather than a build.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it allowed to use an AI chatbot on WhatsApp for my business in 2026?

Yes, for business-specific use. Meta's 2026 terms bar AI providers from distributing general-purpose assistants through the WhatsApp Business Solution, but Meta has said businesses using AI to serve their own customers are not affected. You still need to follow WhatsApp's Business Messaging Policy, including opt-in, opt-out and clear escalation to a human, and Nigeria's data protection law.

### Can I use my existing WhatsApp Business number?

Usually, yes. Meta's coexistence onboarding lets you connect an existing WhatsApp Business app number to the Cloud API and keep using the app for one-to-one chats, with recent chat history shared if you approve it. Some app features are not available on the API side, so review the trade-offs first.

### Will customers know they are talking to a bot?

They should. Being upfront that an assistant handles the first response, and making it easy to reach a person, builds more trust than trying to pass the assistant off as a human. Most customers care more about getting a fast, correct answer than about who gives it.

### Can a WhatsApp AI assistant understand Pidgin and voice notes?

Current AI models can often handle Nigerian Pidgin and mixed English reasonably well, and some setups transcribe voice notes before responding. Accuracy varies by setup, so test with real customer messages before relying on it, and route anything the assistant is unsure about to a person.

### How much does WhatsApp charge for an AI assistant's replies?

Under Meta's pricing from 1 October 2026, each business phone number gets 1,000 free service replies a month, after which service replies are charged per message, and replies inside a free entry point window from a Click-to-WhatsApp ad are not charged. If you use Meta's own Business Agent, its replies are charged per token. Your separate costs are the platform, the AI usage and the setup work.

### Do I need a developer?

For the built-in tools in the WhatsApp Business app, no. For an assistant that answers from your own information, hands over to staff and connects to your CRM, calendar or payment system, you will need either a no-code platform and someone comfortable configuring it, or a technical partner to build and maintain it.
