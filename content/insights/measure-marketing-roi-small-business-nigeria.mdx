You boost a post on Instagram, pay an influencer, print a few hundred flyers and run a Click-to-WhatsApp ad, all in the same month. Sales come in. But if someone asked you which of those four actually paid for itself, could you answer with numbers rather than a feeling?

For most Nigerian small businesses, the honest answer is no. That isn't carelessness. It's the way selling works here. Customers find you on one platform, message you on another, ask questions over three days and pay by bank transfer. No single dashboard sees that whole journey, so the money you spent and the money you made never meet on the same page.

This guide shows you how to measure marketing ROI for a small business in Nigeria in a way that fits how your customers actually buy. You'll learn the four numbers that matter, how to give every channel a trackable "door", how to connect a lead to the naira it eventually paid, and where automation and AI take the manual work out. You can start with a phone and a Google Sheet this week.

## Why your marketing results are so hard to see

Most advice on measuring marketing assumes customers buy through a website checkout, where Google Analytics or Meta's pixel can record the sale. That is not how most Nigerian SMEs sell.

- **Sales close in conversations.** A customer sees your reel, sends a DM, moves to WhatsApp, and pays after a phone call. The platform that showed the ad never sees the payment.
- **Payment happens outside your marketing tools.** A bank transfer alert tells you that money arrived. It doesn't tell you which post, advert or referral brought that customer.
- **Platforms report their own activity, not your profit.** Ads Manager shows reach, clicks and conversations started. Your Google Business Profile shows calls and direction requests. None of them knows your margins, and each one tends to make itself look good.
- **Offline marketing leaves no trail at all.** Flyers, radio mentions, market-day banners and church announcements can work very well, but only if you build a way to trace them.

So the fix is not a fancier dashboard. It's a simple habit: **record where every enquiry came from, and record what every enquiry was eventually worth.** Everything below is a way of doing that reliably.

## The four numbers that actually matter

You don't need twenty metrics. For each marketing channel or campaign, you need these four, worked out every month.

**1. Cost per lead.** What you spent on that channel, divided by the number of genuine enquiries it produced. A "lead" should mean someone who asked about a real product with real intent, not every "hi" or "how much?" that goes quiet.

**2. Lead-to-sale rate.** Of those leads, how many paid? This number tells you as much about your follow-up as it does about your marketing.

**3. Cost per sale.** What you spent, divided by the number of paying customers it produced. This is the number to compare across channels.

**4. Marketing ROI, calculated on gross profit.** The common formula is (revenue from the channel minus marketing cost) divided by marketing cost. The trouble is that revenue isn't what you keep. If you sell a ₦20,000 item that costs you ₦12,000 to buy or make, only ₦8,000 is available to pay for the marketing. So use gross profit:

> Marketing ROI = (gross profit from the channel − marketing cost) ÷ marketing cost

You'll also see **ROAS** (return on ad spend), which is simply revenue divided by ad spend. It's useful for comparing ads, but a ROAS of 2 can still lose money if your margin is thin. ROI on gross profit is the number that tells you whether to keep spending.

### A worked example

*This is an illustrative example, not a real client's figures.*

A Lagos fashion brand spends ₦150,000 on Instagram ads in a month. The ads bring 60 genuine enquiries and 9 sales, worth ₦540,000 in revenue. The brand's average gross margin is 40%, so those sales produced ₦216,000 in gross profit.

- Cost per lead: ₦150,000 ÷ 60 = ₦2,500
- Lead-to-sale rate: 9 ÷ 60 = 15%
- Cost per sale: ₦150,000 ÷ 9 ≈ ₦16,667
- ROAS: ₦540,000 ÷ ₦150,000 = 3.6
- Marketing ROI: (₦216,000 − ₦150,000) ÷ ₦150,000 = 44%

The same month, the brand spends ₦60,000 on flyers, which bring 14 enquiries and 2 sales worth ₦90,000. Gross profit is ₦36,000, so the ROI is (₦36,000 − ₦60,000) ÷ ₦60,000 = −40%. The flyers lost money. Without tracking, both channels would have felt like "marketing that brings people in".

## Step 1: Give every channel its own trackable door

You can't calculate anything if you don't know where each enquiry came from. The goal is to make the source visible at the moment the customer first contacts you, rather than relying on memory later.

**Use a different pre-filled WhatsApp message for each channel.** WhatsApp's [click to chat feature](https://whatsapp.com/faq/en/general/26000030) lets you create a `wa.me` link with your number in international format and a pre-filled message added after `?text=`. The message appears in the customer's chat box, ready to send. Create one link per channel: your Instagram bio link might pre-fill "Hi, I found you on Instagram", your flyer QR code "Hi, I saw your flyer", and your Google Business Profile button "Hi, I found you on Google". When the message arrives, you know the door they came through. Customers can edit the text, so treat this as a strong signal, not a guarantee.

**Tag website links with UTM parameters.** If any of your marketing sends people to a website or landing page, add campaign parameters to the link. Google's [guide to custom campaign URLs](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/10917952) recommends always using `utm_source`, `utm_medium` and `utm_campaign`, and notes that values are case-sensitive, so "Instagram" and "instagram" will show up as two different sources. Pick a naming pattern and stick to it.

**Give offline campaigns their own contact point.** Nigerian entrepreneur Akin Alabi, founder of NairaBet, made this point years ago: [as Nairametrics reported in 2019](https://nairametrics.com/2019/06/21/hey-smes-this-is-why-you-should-track-your-ads-by-akin-alabi/), he advised small businesses to track every advert, and suggested a separate phone number or email address for each campaign so you can see which one brings customers. Today a unique `wa.me` QR code does the same job more cheaply.

**Use campaign-specific codes and offers.** A discount code that only appears in one influencer's post, or "mention RADIO10 for free delivery", turns an untraceable channel into a traceable one.

**Ask, and record the answer.** "Please, how did you hear about us?" is still one of the most useful questions in marketing. Ask it of every new customer and write the answer down in a consistent form.

## Step 2: Keep every lead in one place

Tracking only works if every enquiry lands in a single record. A Google Sheet is enough to begin. Give it these columns:

- Date of first contact
- Customer name and phone number
- Source (Instagram ads, Instagram organic, Google, referral, flyer, influencer, email, walk-in)
- Campaign or specific ad, if known
- What they asked about
- Qualified lead? (yes or no)
- Outcome (paid, lost, still talking)
- Amount paid and date paid

Keep the Source column to a fixed list (use a dropdown) so you never end up with "IG", "insta" and "Instagram" as three different channels.

If your enquiries come mostly through the WhatsApp Business app, its labels (called lists in some newer versions of the app) help you mark chats as new lead, paid and so on as you go. They make the daily work easier, but they won't add up your numbers. The sheet, or a CRM, is still your record.

Because this record holds customers' names and phone numbers, treat it as personal data under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023: limit who can open it, don't share it around in group chats, and only use it for the purpose the customer would expect.

## Step 3: Close the loop when the money arrives

This is the step most businesses skip, and it's the one that makes the rest worthwhile. When a customer pays, find their row and fill in the outcome, the amount and the date. When a lead says no or goes cold, mark it lost.

Make one person responsible for this, and make it part of confirming any payment. "Payment confirmed, sheet updated" should be a single habit, not two separate jobs. If you already send payment reminders or order confirmations, update the record at the same moment. Our guides to [automating payment reminders](/insights/automate-payment-reminders-nigeria) and [WhatsApp order confirmations](/insights/whatsapp-order-confirmation-automation-nigeria) show the points in the process where that update naturally belongs.

## Step 4: Count all of your marketing costs

Undercounting cost is the quickest way to fool yourself. For each channel, include:

- Ad spend and boosted posts (check the actual charges on your card or account, including any bank charges on foreign-currency payments)
- Influencer or content creator fees, including free products you gave away
- Design, photography and video costs
- Printing and distribution for offline materials
- Tools and subscriptions used mainly for that channel

If you pay a staff member or agency mainly to run one channel, add a fair share of that cost too. You don't need to be perfect. You need to be consistent from month to month.

## Step 5: Run the numbers monthly and decide

At the end of each month, filter your sheet by source and work out the four numbers for each channel. A pivot table in Google Sheets will do the counting for you. Then make one decision per channel:

- **Positive ROI and enough volume:** keep it, and test whether more budget holds the same cost per sale.
- **Positive ROI but tiny volume:** keep it, but don't expect it to carry growth.
- **Negative ROI with lots of leads:** the marketing is working but the selling isn't. Look at your reply speed, pricing and follow-up before you cut the channel.
- **Negative ROI with few leads:** cut it, or change one thing and test again for a fixed period.

Give each channel a fair test period before you judge it. A single week is rarely enough, especially when customers take days to decide.

## Where automation removes the manual work

The manual system works, but it depends on people typing things in during a busy day. As your volume grows, that's where records break down. Automation fixes the weak points:

- **Leads arrive in the record by themselves.** Website forms, Facebook lead forms and other web enquiries can be sent straight into a sheet or CRM with the source already filled in. Our guide to [automating Facebook lead ads follow-up](/insights/automate-facebook-lead-ads-follow-up) shows this for Meta's Instant Forms.
- **WhatsApp sources are captured automatically.** On the WhatsApp Business Platform, an automation can read the first message, recognise which pre-filled text it matches, and tag the lead with its source, so nobody has to remember.
- **Payments update the record.** Payment providers can notify your system when a payment succeeds, which marks the lead as paid with the correct amount, without anyone retyping it.
- **Reports build themselves.** A weekly summary of cost per lead and cost per sale by channel can arrive in your inbox or WhatsApp every Monday.

There's a further benefit once your sales data is connected. Meta's ad system can only optimise for what you tell it. Meta's published case study of **Thành Vinh Holdings**, a Vietnamese ecommerce group selling cosmetics in Malaysia, describes how the business used Meta's Conversions API through its partner Pancake and ran Click-to-WhatsApp ads optimised for purchases rather than messages. [According to Meta](https://whatsappbusiness.com/resources/success-stories/thanhvinhholdings/), an A/B test in Ads Manager for a 31 October to 14 November 2024 campaign showed a 1.8X increase in sales and a 40% decrease in cost per purchase compared with its usual message-optimised ads. That is a vendor-published result from another market, not a promise. The lesson that transfers is simple: **once your system knows which leads became sales, your ad platforms can learn from it too.** Our [Click-to-WhatsApp ads guide](/insights/click-to-whatsapp-ads-nigeria) explains that connection in more detail.

## How AI helps (and where to be careful)

AI is genuinely useful in measurement, mostly for the messy, text-heavy parts:

- **Tidying sources.** Paste a column of free-text "how did you hear about us" answers and ask an AI assistant to map each one to your fixed source list. Check the results, then fix the originals.
- **Explaining the month.** Give it your monthly totals by channel and ask for a plain-English summary of what changed and what to test next. It's a fast first draft for a management update.
- **Finding patterns in lost leads.** Summarise why lost leads said no (price, delivery area, timing), then ask the AI to group the reasons. Those groups often point to the real problem faster than the numbers do.

Be careful with customer data. Don't paste names and phone numbers into a public AI tool. Remove personal details first, or use a tool your business has approved for that data. And always check the arithmetic yourself. AI writing tools can make calculation mistakes, and a wrong ROI figure leads to a wrong budget decision.

## Mistakes to avoid

**Judging channels by likes, followers or cost per click.** These are signs of attention, not profit. A channel with expensive clicks can still have the cheapest cost per sale.

**Using revenue instead of gross profit.** This makes every channel look better than it is, especially for low-margin products.

**Counting a sale twice.** If a customer saw your Instagram ad and then searched for you on Google, record one primary source per sale (usually the first contact) and note the other in a comments column. Don't add the sale to both channels' totals.

**Writing off "brand" channels too quickly.** Some marketing, such as useful content or community work, takes months to show up. Track it, but judge it over a longer period than a direct-response ad.

**Letting the sheet go stale.** A record that's updated only when someone remembers is worse than useless, because it gives you false confidence. Make updates part of the daily routine, or automate them.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a good marketing ROI for a small business?

Any ROI above zero, calculated on gross profit, means the channel paid for itself. Beyond that, there's no universal benchmark worth trusting, because margins, prices and repeat-purchase rates vary so much between businesses. Compare your channels against each other and against your own previous months.

### How long should I run a campaign before judging it?

Long enough to see a pattern, and long enough for slower buyers to decide. For most small businesses that means at least two to four weeks for paid ads, and longer for content, SEO and referral programmes. Decide the test period before you start, so you don't cut a campaign on a bad first week.

### Can I measure ROI on organic social media posts?

Yes, with the same method. Use a dedicated `wa.me` link or code in your bio and posts, record the source on every enquiry, and count the time and production costs as your marketing cost. Organic isn't free; it's paid for in time.

### Do I need Google Analytics to measure marketing ROI?

Not if most of your sales happen on WhatsApp, by phone or in person. Analytics helps when your website is part of the journey, and UTM-tagged links make it far more useful. But the lead record that connects each enquiry to a payment is what actually gives you ROI.

### How do I track flyers, radio or word of mouth?

Give each one its own contact point: a unique QR code that opens a `wa.me` link with a specific pre-filled message, a campaign-only code, or a separate line. For word of mouth, ask every new customer who referred them, and record it.

### What if a customer heard about us from several places?

That's normal. Record the first source they mention as the primary one, note others separately, and stay consistent. Perfect attribution isn't the goal. Making better budget decisions than you could last month is.
