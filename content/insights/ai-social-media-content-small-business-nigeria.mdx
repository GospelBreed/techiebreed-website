You know you should post more. You also know what happens in practice: a good week of posts when things are quiet, then nothing for three weeks because orders, staff and suppliers came first. When you finally sit down to post, you stare at a blank caption box and end up sharing another product photo with "DM to order".

AI tools can fix most of that, but not in the way the hype suggests. They will not run your page for you, and a feed full of generic AI captions will not sell anything in Lagos, Kano or Port Harcourt. What they do very well is take the slow parts of content work off your plate: turning ideas into a plan, a plan into drafts, and one good photo or video into several posts.

This guide shows you how to use AI to create social media content for a small business in Nigeria as a simple monthly system: one planning session, one batch-writing session and short weekly check-ins. It covers the prompts to use, how to keep your own voice, Meta's free scheduling tools, the Nigerian rules that apply to promotional posts, and how to tell whether any of it is bringing in customers. It is written for owners and marketers who want to do this themselves.

## What AI can and can't do for your social media

It helps to be clear about this before you start, because most disappointment with AI content comes from expecting the wrong things.

**AI is good at:** brainstorming post ideas around a theme, turning a rough idea into a first draft, rewriting one caption for different platforms, suggesting hooks for short videos, summarising customer questions into topics, resizing and tidying images, and adapting content for occasions such as Sallah, Easter, back-to-school or Black Friday.

**AI is poor at:** knowing your prices, stock and delivery terms; knowing what actually happened in your business this week; sounding like you without being told how you sound; judging what will offend or confuse your particular customers; and checking whether a claim is true or allowed.

So the rule for this whole system is simple. **You supply the facts, the voice and the judgement. AI supplies the speed.** Every step below is built around that split.

## Before you open any AI tool: write a one-page brand brief

The single biggest improvement you can make to AI content is to stop starting from scratch. A short brand brief, saved in a document and pasted into the AI at the start of every session, gives it the context it would otherwise make up.

Keep it to one page:

- **What you sell and to whom.** "Home-cooked soups and stews in bulk for busy families and small offices in Lekki and Ajah."
- **Your customers' main worries.** Freshness, delivery time, portion size, whether they can trust you with a transfer.
- **How you talk.** Warm but brief? Playful? Formal? Do you use Pidgin? List five words or phrases you would use, and five you never would.
- **Facts the AI must never invent.** Prices, delivery areas and fees, opening hours, payment options, refund terms. Paste the current versions.
- **What you want people to do.** Usually: tap your WhatsApp link, visit your website or come to your shop.

There is a good public example of why this matters. In a November 2026 report for the Jefferson City News Tribune, journalist Salena Zito described how April Polk, who runs the US fashion brand JaeLuxe, set up Meta's new AI agent for small businesses. Her first step was not asking for posts. It was uploading her two-page brand guide covering fonts, voice and customers, which the tool then expanded after reviewing her social accounts ([source](https://www.newstribune.com/news/2026/nov/06/commentary-while-the-public-fears-ai-these-ohio/)). The same article notes that both owners it featured had close ties to Meta's small-business programmes and still reviewed the tool's work before anything went out, so treat it as an illustration of good practice rather than proof of results. The lesson travels well: the tool can only sound like you if you tell it who you are.

## Step 1: Decide what your posts are for

"Post consistently" is not a goal. Before you plan a month of content, write down what you want social media to do for the business in the next 30 days. Pick one or two:

- More WhatsApp enquiries about a specific product or service
- More bookings for a slot-based service (a salon, clinic or training class)
- More people visiting your shop or ordering for an upcoming season
- Trust with new buyers who have never bought from you

Then choose three or four **content pillars**: the recurring types of post that serve those goals. For most Nigerian small businesses, a mix like this works:

1. **Show the product or service working.** Before-and-after, behind-the-scenes, how it is made, how it is packed.
2. **Answer the questions buyers ask.** Delivery, payment, sizes, ingredients, how long a service takes. Your WhatsApp and Instagram inbox is the best source of these.
3. **Proof.** Real reviews and real customer stories, shared with permission.
4. **Offers and timely posts.** New stock, seasonal menus, promos with clear end dates.

## Step 2: Ask AI to draft a monthly content calendar

Now open your AI assistant (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or similar all work for this), paste your brand brief and ask for a plan, not posts. A prompt like this works well:

> Using the brand brief above, plan 12 social media posts for the next four weeks for Instagram and Facebook. Use these pillars: [list]. Our goal this month is [goal]. Important dates in this period: [dates]. For each post give: date, pillar, format (photo, carousel, Reel or Story), a one-line idea, the photo or video we would need to take, and the call to action. Present it as a table. Do not invent prices, offers or customer stories.

Read the plan critically. Delete ideas that do not suit you, move posts around your real calendar (market days, payday weekends, holidays, staff availability), and replace any vague idea with something specific to your business. Then copy the final table into a Google Sheet with extra columns for **caption**, **visual**, **status** and **result**. That sheet is now your content calendar and your approval tracker in one place.

Three or four posts a week that you can actually sustain will do more than a daily plan you abandon after a fortnight.

## Step 3: Batch-write the captions

Set aside one session to draft every caption for the month. Paste the brand brief again, then work through the sheet a week at a time:

> Write captions for the posts in rows 1 to 3. Keep each under 80 words, start with a line that would make our customer stop scrolling, use the voice described in the brief, and end with this call to action: "Tap the WhatsApp link in our bio and send PRICE LIST". Use only the facts in the brief.

A few habits make the drafts much better:

**Feed it real material.** Paste three customer questions from your inbox, a voice-note transcript from your chef or a short description of last week's busiest order. Specific input produces specific captions; generic input produces "Elevate your experience with our premium quality products".

**Show the price.** If a post is about a product, put the price, or a "from" price, in the caption. Asking people to "DM for price" wastes conversations and, as we explain in our [guide to automating Instagram DMs](/insights/automate-instagram-dms-nigeria), it may also conflict with the price-display rules in Nigeria's consumer protection law.

**Ask for options, then choose.** Request three versions of a hook and pick the one that sounds most like you. Editing is faster than writing, and choosing keeps your judgement in the loop.

**Be careful with Pidgin and local languages.** AI tools can draft in Pidgin, Yoruba, Hausa or Igbo, but the results are uneven and can read as stiff or slightly wrong to native speakers. Have someone who speaks the language naturally check every post before it goes out.

**Adapt, don't copy, across platforms.** Ask the AI to turn one caption into a shorter Story version, a WhatsApp Status line and a LinkedIn version for business buyers. Each platform has different habits.

## Step 4: Create the visuals, mostly from your own photos

Customers buying from a small business want to see the real thing. Use AI to make your own photos and videos work harder, not to replace them.

- **Take the raw material yourself.** A phone, good daylight and a clean background beat most generated images for products, food and services.
- **Use AI design tools for the finishing.** Canva and similar tools can remove backgrounds, resize one design for feed, Story and WhatsApp Status, and apply your brand colours and fonts consistently.
- **Use AI for short-video help.** Ask your AI assistant for a 15-second Reel script and shot list, then film it yourself. Free AI video tools can add captions and trims; as a [Daily Trust guide to free AI video tools](https://dailytrust.com/?p=1971007) advises, check for watermarks and export limits before you rely on one for business content.
- **Use generated images for backgrounds and graphics, not for your product.** An AI-generated "photo" of a dress, cake or room you do not actually sell misleads buyers and creates complaints later.

## Step 5: Review every post before it is scheduled

This is the step most people skip and the one that protects your reputation. Before a post moves from "draft" to "approved" in your sheet, check:

1. **Facts.** Prices, delivery fees, dates, opening hours and offer terms match the brand brief and today's reality.
2. **Claims.** No "best in Nigeria", health promises or before-and-after results you cannot back up.
3. **Permission.** You have the customer's permission to share their photo, name, review or chat screenshot. The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 applies to personal information you collect from customers, and asking first is the simple, safe habit.
4. **Rights.** You are allowed to use any music, images or fonts in the post.
5. **Voice.** Read it aloud. If it does not sound like something you would say to a customer, rewrite it.

### The rules worth knowing

**Meta's AI labels.** According to [Meta's help centre](https://www.meta.com/help/artificial-intelligence/1783222608822690/), Facebook, Instagram and Threads may require you to label content that contains photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created or altered, including with AI, and there may be penalties for leaving the label off when it is required. Meta says this requirement does not apply to images, although images can still be labelled automatically if its systems detect AI signals. For ads, Meta [says](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/02/gen-ai-transparency-metas-ads-products/) it applies "AI info" labels to ads made or significantly edited with its own generative AI tools and has started detecting ads made with third-party AI tools through industry-standard signals. The practical habit: if a Reel uses an AI voiceover or realistic AI video, label it.

**ARCON and advertising approval.** The Advertising Regulatory Council of Nigeria (ARCON) says adverts must be vetted and approved before they are exposed to the public, and in May 2025 it [extended that warning explicitly to social media, influencers and content creators](https://www.premiumtimesng.com/news/top-news/792469-arcon-bars-agencies-influencers-from-advertising-without-approval-warns-against-deceptive-promotions.html). [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/?p=1103055) has reported that many small businesses still run social media ads without approval and that platforms such as Meta and Google do not ask for ARCON certificates. How far the rules reach for ordinary business posts is debated among lawyers. We are not lawyers: if you run paid ads, use influencers or make strong promotional claims, check the current requirements with ARCON or a legal adviser.

## Step 6: Schedule a month at a time in Meta Business Suite

You do not need a paid scheduling tool to start. Meta Business Suite is free and lets you schedule Facebook Page and Instagram posts, Stories and Reels, then see them in the Planner, a calendar of everything scheduled and published. Scheduled times can be set from about 20 minutes to 29 days ahead, which suits a monthly routine: in the last week of each month, schedule the next month's approved posts.

Two limits to plan around. Meta Business Suite only covers Facebook and Instagram, so TikTok, LinkedIn and X need their own apps or a third-party scheduler; our guide on [how to use TikTok for business in Nigeria](/insights/how-to-use-tiktok-for-business-nigeria) covers TikTok's own tools and rules. And you still need someone watching comments and messages after each post goes live: a scheduled post that gets ten questions nobody answers is worse than no post. Our guides to [automating Instagram DMs](/insights/automate-instagram-dms-nigeria) and [sending WhatsApp broadcast messages properly](/insights/whatsapp-broadcast-messages-business-nigeria) cover what happens after the post does its job.

## Step 7: Measure enquiries and sales, then let AI help you improve

Likes are pleasant. Enquiries and sales pay the bills. To know which content works, every post needs a traceable next step:

- Use a **different WhatsApp keyword per campaign** ("Send SOUP10", "Send BOOKING") so you can tell which post produced each chat.
- Fill in the **result** column in your content sheet every week: comments, saves and shares from Meta's insights, plus the number of chats and orders that came from each post.
- At the end of the month, paste the sheet into your AI assistant and ask: "Which pillars and formats produced the most enquiries and orders? What should we do more of, and what should we drop next month?" Then check its reasoning against your own sense of what happened.

For the full method of tying chats and payments back to each channel, see our guide to [measuring marketing ROI for a small business in Nigeria](/insights/measure-marketing-roi-small-business-nigeria). If a post performs well organically, it is often the best candidate to put money behind; our guide to [Click-to-WhatsApp ads](/insights/click-to-whatsapp-ads-nigeria) explains how to do that and judge it by cost per sale.

## Where automation fits

Once the routine works by hand, parts of it can run on their own. Common next steps include a form or sheet where staff drop photos and post ideas, an automation that sends each new idea to an AI tool for a first draft and adds it to the calendar, an approval notification to the owner before anything is scheduled, and an automatic weekly summary of which posts produced chats and orders. These can be built with no-code tools that connect your sheet, AI assistant and messaging apps. Keep the human approval step: nothing should publish without a person checking it.

## An illustrative month

*This is an illustrative walkthrough, not a client case study or a real result.*

A small catering business in Ibadan posts whenever the owner remembers, usually photos of trays with "DM to order". She writes a one-page brief: bulk jollof, small chops and soups for office lunches and family events, prices from her current list, delivery within Ibadan, and a friendly tone with some Pidgin.

On the last Saturday of the month she spends an hour with an AI assistant planning 12 posts across four pillars: how dishes are prepared, answers to common questions (minimum order, delivery fee, how far ahead to book), real customer reviews she has permission to share, and an end-of-month office lunch offer with a clear end date. On Sunday she batch-writes the captions with AI, edits them so they sound like her, and asks her nephew to check the Pidgin lines. During the week she takes photos and short clips while cooking, uses a design tool to resize them for feed and Status, and schedules everything in Meta Business Suite.

Every post ends with a WhatsApp keyword. At the end of the month her sheet shows which posts led to enquiries and orders, and that becomes the starting point for next month's plan.

## Mistakes to avoid

**Publishing AI drafts unedited.** Followers notice generic captions quickly, and they stop reading.

**Letting AI invent facts.** Prices, offers, reviews and statistics must come from you, not from the model.

**Using AI images of products you don't sell.** It creates expectations your real product cannot meet.

**Planning more than you can produce.** Start with three posts a week. Increase only when the routine is easy.

**Ignoring comments and messages.** Content creates conversations. Make sure someone answers them.

**Measuring only engagement.** A post with 40 likes and five orders beats one with 400 likes and none.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which AI tool is best for social media content?

For planning and captions, general AI assistants such as ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini all work well when you give them a good brand brief. For visuals, design tools with built-in AI features handle resizing, backgrounds and templates. The habit of briefing, editing and reviewing matters far more than the specific tool.

### Is it okay to post AI-generated content on Facebook and Instagram?

Yes, within Meta's rules. Meta may require you to label photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio created or altered with AI, and may label content automatically when it detects AI signals. Meta says images do not currently require a label from you.

### Can AI write in Pidgin or Nigerian languages?

It can draft in them, but quality varies. Always have a fluent speaker review the text before posting.

### How often should a small business post?

Consistency matters more than volume. Three or four useful posts a week, every week, is a realistic target for most small businesses using this system.

### Will AI content hurt my reach?

We are not aware of Meta saying it reduces reach simply because a post was made with AI tools. Content that is generic, unoriginal or ignored by your audience performs poorly whichever way it was made.

### Do I need ARCON approval for my social media posts?

ARCON says adverts on social media need prior approval, but how that applies to everyday business posts is debated. If you run paid ads, use influencers or make strong promotional claims, check the current requirements with ARCON or a legal adviser.
