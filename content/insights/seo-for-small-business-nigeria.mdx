Most small business owners in Nigeria meet SEO the same way: an unsolicited message promising "page one of Google in two weeks", or a website that cost good money and has brought in almost nobody since launch. Neither tells you what SEO actually involves, or whether it is worth doing yourself.

It usually is. Google is where Nigerians search: StatCounter's figures put Google's share of search in Nigeria at [98.28% in September 2026](https://gs.statcounter.com/search-engine-market-share/all-worldwide/nigeria), and DataReportal's [Digital 2026 report](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-nigeria) counted about 109 million internet users in the country in October 2025. Some of those people are typing in exactly what you sell, right now.

This guide shows you how to do SEO for a small business in Nigeria, step by step: checking whether Google can see you, finding the searches your customers really make, building pages that deserve to rank, using AI without breaking Google's rules, and, the part most guides skip, measuring whether search is bringing in paying customers.

## What SEO means for a small business (and what it doesn't)

SEO, search engine optimisation, is the work of helping search engines understand your website and helping the right people find it. Google's own [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) is refreshingly honest about it: there are no secrets that will automatically rank a site first.

For a small business, search visibility comes from three places on the same results page:

- **The map results**, powered by your Google Business Profile. This matters most for local, "near me" style searches.
- **The normal web results**, powered by your website's pages.
- **AI Overviews and AI Mode**, the AI-generated summaries Google shows for some searches, which link to supporting websites.

The good news is that one set of habits serves all three. The bad news, if you were hoping for a shortcut, is that those habits are mostly about being genuinely useful and technically findable, not about tricks.

### Set honest expectations first

Google's starter guide says some changes show up within hours and others take several months, and suggests waiting a few weeks before judging whether a change worked. In practice, a small site should expect to win first on specific, lower-competition searches ("commercial cleaning company in Wuse 2") long before broad ones ("cleaning services Nigeria"). Plan for months, not days, and treat anyone who guarantees a ranking with suspicion. If you need enquiries sooner, paid search can fill the gap while SEO builds; our guide on [how to run Google Ads in Nigeria](/insights/how-to-run-google-ads-nigeria) covers billing, setup and tracking.

## Step 1: Check whether Google can see you, and set up Search Console

Before changing anything, find out where you stand. Type `site:yourbusiness.com` into Google. If pages from your site appear, Google has indexed at least part of it. If nothing appears, that is the first problem to solve.

Next, set up **Google Search Console**. It's free, and it's the only place that shows how Google actually sees your site: which searches you appear for, how often people click, and which pages have problems.

1. Go to Search Console and choose **Add property**.
2. Pick the **Domain** option if you can. Google's [help page](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/34592) explains that a Domain property covers every version of your site (with and without "www", http and https) and is verified with a DNS record. You add a short TXT record at whoever manages your domain, your registrar or hosting company, then click Verify.
3. Once verified, open **Sitemaps** and submit your sitemap address. Most website builders and WordPress SEO plugins create one automatically, often at `/sitemap.xml`.

Do this even if you plan to pay someone else to handle SEO. The account should belong to the business, not to a freelancer, for the same reason your domain should.

## Step 2: Find the searches your customers actually make

Most small business websites are written in the owner's language ("bespoke facility management solutions"). Customers search in theirs ("office cleaning company Abuja price"). Keyword research closes that gap, and you don't need an expensive tool to start.

**Start with your own inbox.** Your WhatsApp chats, DMs, calls and emails are the best keyword list you have. Write down the exact questions people ask before they buy: "how much is…", "do you cover Lekki?", "can you deliver to Ibadan?", "how long does it take?". Those questions are what people type into Google before they message anyone.

**Then check Google itself.** Type the start of a search and note the autocomplete suggestions. Look at the "People also ask" box and the related searches at the bottom of the page. Note the place names customers use: neighbourhoods and LGAs, not just "Lagos".

**Once Search Console has data,** its Performance report shows the actual searches your pages already appear for. That's often where the quickest wins are: searches where you show up on page two and could move up with a better page.

AI can help here. Paste a batch of customer questions (with names and phone numbers removed, since those are personal data under the Nigeria Data Protection Act) into an AI assistant and ask it to group them by topic and buying stage. You get a tidy list of themes in minutes. Check it against what you know of your customers; the AI only sees the text you gave it.

For each service, settle on **one main search phrase** and a handful of related ones. That's what the page in the next step is built around.

## Step 3: Build one strong page for each service

The most common reason small business sites don't rank is that there's nothing on them to rank. A home page with a slogan and a "services" page listing six things in one line gives Google very little to work with.

Give each main service its own page. A good service page:

- **Answers the searcher's real questions:** what's included, who it's for, the areas you cover, how long it takes, how to book, and what it costs or how pricing works. Naira prices or ranges, where you can share them, answer the question most Nigerian customers ask first.
- **Shows proof:** real photos of your work, your registration details where relevant, and genuine reviews. Never invent testimonials.
- **Has a clear title and description.** Google's starter guide recommends a descriptive, unique title for each page and a short meta description summarising it. "Office Cleaning in Abuja | [Business name]" beats "Services".
- **Uses headings that match the questions**, so both readers and Google can scan it.
- **Makes the next step easy:** a WhatsApp button, a phone number that's answered, or a short enquiry form.
- **Links to related pages** on your own site with descriptive link text, which helps Google find and understand them.

### Location pages: real areas only

If you genuinely serve several areas, a page that explains how you serve each one (travel times, local projects, the team covering it) can help. What doesn't help is copying the same page 30 times and swapping "Ikeja" for "Ikoyi". Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) call this **doorway abuse**: pages created to rank for similar searches that lead people to intermediate pages less useful than the final destination. It is one of the most common shortcuts in Nigerian SEO, and one of the riskiest.

## Step 4: Fix the technical basics that matter on a Nigerian phone

You don't need to become a developer, but a few technical basics decide whether Google can use your site and whether visitors stay.

- **Mobile first.** Most of your visitors will be on a phone, often on mobile data. Open your own site on an ordinary Android phone, off Wi-Fi, and try to find a price and send an enquiry. If that's frustrating, it's frustrating for customers too.
- **Speed.** Compress large images before uploading them and avoid heavy sliders and auto-playing videos. Google's free PageSpeed Insights tool shows what is slowing a page down.
- **HTTPS.** Your site should load with the padlock. Most Nigerian hosts include a free SSL certificate.
- **Nothing blocked by accident.** Search Console's page indexing report shows pages Google couldn't index and why. Check it monthly.
- **Your domain.** Google's guidance on [multi-regional sites](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites) describes country-code domains as a strong signal that a site is meant for a particular country. A .com.ng domain therefore suits a business serving only Nigeria. A .com works too, especially if you also serve diaspora customers. Don't change an established domain just for this.

## Step 5: Connect your Google Business Profile

For any business customers visit, or that visits customers, the map results are often where the first enquiries come from. Your Google Business Profile and your website should support each other: the same business name, address and phone number on both, the profile linking to the right page on your site, and your service pages mentioning the areas you serve.

We've covered this in detail in our guide on [how to optimise your Google Business Profile in Nigeria](/insights/google-business-profile-nigeria), including verification, reviews within Google's rules and the WhatsApp chat button. If you only have time for one thing this week, start there.

## Step 6: Publish helpful content, and use AI the right way

Once your service pages are solid, articles that answer customer questions bring in people earlier in their decision. A cleaning company might write "How often should an office be deep cleaned?"; a solar installer, "What size inverter do I need for a three-bedroom flat?". These earn visits, build trust and give you pages to link to your services from.

This is where AI tempts people most, so be clear about the rules. Google's [guidance on generative AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content) says AI can be particularly useful for researching a topic and adding structure to original content. The same guidance warns that using AI to generate many pages without adding value for users may violate its spam policy on **scaled content abuse**, and it stresses checking accuracy before publishing.

A workflow that stays on the right side of that line:

1. **Start with what only you know:** the question customers ask, your answer, real examples from jobs you've done, your prices and the mistakes you see people make.
2. **Ask AI for an outline and the questions you might have missed.**
3. **Write or heavily edit the draft yourself**, keeping your examples and opinions in.
4. **Check every fact, figure and claim** before it goes live, and remove anything you can't stand behind.
5. **Publish fewer, better pieces.** One genuinely useful article a fortnight beats twenty generic ones in a week.

A simple test before publishing: could someone with no knowledge of your business have produced this page with a single prompt? If yes, it needs more of you in it.

## Step 7: Earn links and mentions the clean way

Google finds pages largely through links from other pages, and links from relevant, trusted sites help show that your business is real and worth recommending. For a small business, the realistic sources are:

- your trade association, professional body or chamber of commerce member directory
- suppliers, partners and clients who list who they work with
- local news or industry coverage of something genuinely newsworthy, like a new branch, a community project or useful data you've gathered
- reputable business directories where real customers look

What to avoid: buying links. Google's spam policies list buying or selling links for ranking purposes, including paying for posts that contain links, as **link spam**. Sponsored articles on Nigerian blogs and news sites are a normal marketing channel, but Google's policies say paid links are acceptable only when they're marked with `rel="sponsored"` or `rel="nofollow"`. Pay for the audience, not for a ranking boost.

## Step 8: Turn search visitors into enquiries, and measure it

Traffic isn't the goal. Customers are. This is where SEO connects to the rest of your marketing and operations, and where a little automation pays off.

**Make every enquiry traceable.** Give your website's WhatsApp button its own pre-filled message, such as "Hi, I found you on your website and I'd like to ask about…", so you know which chats came from the site. Send form submissions straight into one place, a CRM or even a shared sheet, rather than to someone's personal inbox. Ask new callers how they found you and record the answer.

**Watch the right numbers in Search Console each month:** impressions (how often you appeared), clicks, click-through rate, and which searches and pages are growing. Google's guidance on [AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) notes that visits from AI Overviews and AI Mode are included in your overall Search Console traffic, so you don't need a separate tool for those.

**Close the loop to sales.** Count how many enquiries from search became paying customers, and what they were worth. Our guide to [measuring marketing ROI for a small business in Nigeria](/insights/measure-marketing-roi-small-business-nigeria) walks through a simple method that works even when customers pay by bank transfer.

**Automate the report.** Rather than someone logging into three tools every month, a simple workflow can pull Search Console figures and enquiry counts into one sheet or dashboard and send it to the owner automatically. If you're new to setting up workflows like this, our step-by-step guide on [how to automate business processes in Nigeria](/insights/how-to-automate-business-processes-nigeria) shows how to choose and build your first one.

## A real example: what fixing the basics did for one site

Google publishes a [case study](https://developers.google.com/search/case-studies/saramin-case-study) about Saramin, a South Korean job site. In 2015 Saramin registered its site in Search Console and spent the first year finding and fixing crawling problems the tool reported. Google reports that this alone produced a 15% increase in organic traffic.

The team then followed Google's SEO and Search Console guidelines more closely: removing unhelpful meta tags, dealing with duplicate content and adding structured data. According to the case study, organic traffic from Google during the September 2019 hiring season was double the previous year's, new member sign-ups from Google organic traffic rose 93%, and the conversion rate from that traffic rose 9% year on year.

Saramin is a large platform, not a small business, and its results came over several years, so treat them as an illustration rather than a forecast. The transferable lesson is the order of work: make sure Google can crawl and understand the site, fix what Search Console flags, then improve steadily. No tricks were involved.

## An illustrative 90-day plan: a cleaning company in Abuja

*This is an illustrative example, not a TechieBreed client.*

A five-person commercial cleaning company serves offices in Wuse, Maitama, Garki and Jabi. It has a three-page website, no Search Console account and gets nearly all its work through referrals.

1. **Weeks 1–2:** verify the domain in Search Console and submit the sitemap; fix the Google Business Profile; list the 20 questions prospects ask most often on WhatsApp.
2. **Weeks 3–6:** replace the single services page with four service pages (office cleaning, post-construction cleaning, deep cleaning, janitorial contracts), each with photos of real jobs, what's included, pricing guidance, areas served and a WhatsApp button with its own pre-filled message.
3. **Weeks 7–10:** publish two articles answering frequent questions, written by the owner with AI help on structure, and ask two suppliers and a business association to list the company.
4. **Weeks 11–13:** review Search Console for searches where the pages appear on page two, improve those pages, and set up a monthly automated report showing search clicks, website enquiries and contracts won.

Nothing here is complicated. The difference comes from doing each step properly and keeping it going after the first three months.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Paying for guarantees.** Nobody controls Google's rankings. Ask any SEO provider what they will do, how they'll report it and who will own the accounts.
- **Building near-identical pages for every neighbourhood.** That's doorway abuse, not local SEO.
- **Publishing AI text in bulk.** It risks scaled content abuse and does little for real customers.
- **Buying "high-DA backlinks" packages.** Paid links that aren't marked as sponsored break Google's link spam policy.
- **Forgetting the phone.** A beautiful desktop site that's slow and fiddly on mobile data loses most of its visitors.
- **Measuring rankings instead of revenue.** A first-page position for a search nobody buys from is worth less than a fifth-place position for one that brings contracts.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does SEO take for a small business in Nigeria?

Google's own guidance says changes can take anywhere from a few hours to several months to show, and suggests waiting a few weeks before judging a change. For a new or neglected site, plan on several months of steady work, with specific, local searches improving before broader ones.

### Can I do SEO myself without hiring an agency?

Yes, for most small businesses. The core work (Search Console, service pages, a Google Business Profile, helpful articles and measurement) needs time and consistency more than specialist tools. Bring in help for technical problems, larger sites or when your time is better spent elsewhere.

### Do I need a website for SEO, or is a Google Business Profile enough?

A Google Business Profile alone can win local map searches. But a website gives you pages that can rank for specific services and questions, a place to show prices and proof, and a way to measure enquiries properly. For most businesses, the two work best together.

### Can I use ChatGPT or other AI tools to write my website content?

You can use AI to help research, outline and edit, and Google's guidance allows that. What it warns against is using AI to mass-produce pages that add no value, which can fall under its scaled content abuse policy. Keep your own knowledge and examples at the centre, and check every fact before publishing.

### Is a .com.ng domain better than .com for SEO in Nigeria?

Google treats country-code domains as a strong signal that a site targets that country, so .com.ng suits a business that only serves Nigeria. A .com can rank in Nigeria too, and may suit businesses with international customers. Don't move an established domain just for SEO.

### Do I need to do anything special to appear in Google's AI Overviews?

According to Google, no. Its guidance on AI features says there are no extra requirements or special optimisations beyond normal SEO best practice. A page needs to be indexed and eligible to appear with a snippet in Search. Clear, helpful, accurate content is still what counts.
