A new client says yes on Monday. By Friday your team has sent the engagement letter twice (the first went to the wrong email), asked for the CAC documents three times on WhatsApp, created a Google Drive folder that nobody else can find, and still not raised the first invoice. The client is wondering whether they made the right choice, and the work you were hired for hasn't started.

That is what manual client onboarding looks like in many Nigerian accounting, tax, legal, consulting, HR and marketing firms. Nobody is lazy. The steps simply live in different people's heads, inboxes and phones.

This guide explains how to automate client onboarding in Nigeria step by step: what to automate, which tools fit a small firm, how to handle documents, e-signatures and payments, what Nigerian data protection and anti-money-laundering rules mean for your intake, and where a person should stay in charge. It is written for firm owners, practice managers and operations leads, not developers.

## What client onboarding actually involves

Client onboarding is everything between "yes, let's work together" and "the work has started". For most professional service firms it covers the same six jobs:

1. **Collecting client details**: names, contacts, company information, the service they've bought.
2. **Collecting documents**: ID, CAC certificate and status report, TIN, proof of address, prior-year accounts, contracts, whatever your service needs.
3. **Agreeing terms**: an engagement letter or service agreement, signed.
4. **Getting paid**: a deposit, retainer or first invoice.
5. **Setting up internally**: a client record, a folder, a job or matter, an owner, tasks for the team.
6. **Kicking off**: a welcome message, a first meeting, and a clear "here's what happens next".

Each job is simple. The trouble is the hand-offs between them. Someone has to remember to send the letter after the details arrive, chase the documents, raise the invoice once the letter is signed, and book the kickoff once the payment lands. When that "someone" is busy, onboarding stalls.

## Why onboarding goes wrong in Nigerian firms

A few patterns come up again and again:

- **WhatsApp as the filing cabinet.** Clients send passport photos, utility bills and CAC documents as WhatsApp images to whichever staff member they know. Finding them later, or proving what was received, is painful.
- **Payment by transfer with no link to the client.** A deposit arrives as a bank alert with a name that doesn't match the company. Someone has to work out who paid before the work can start.
- **Chasing by memory.** There's no list of outstanding documents per client, so follow-ups depend on a staff member remembering.
- **Every client gets a slightly different process.** One partner sends a PDF letter by email, another prints it for a wet signature, a third forgets it altogether.
- **No view of the pipeline.** The owner can't see how many new clients are stuck, or at which step.

The result is slower starts, delayed fees and a poor first impression at the exact moment the client is deciding whether to trust you.

## How to automate client onboarding in Nigeria: seven steps

### Step 1: Write down your onboarding checklist, per service

Start on paper, not in a tool. For each service you sell (monthly bookkeeping, annual audit support, company secretarial, a legal retainer, a marketing retainer), list:

- the details you need from the client;
- the documents you need, and which are mandatory before work starts;
- the agreement they must sign;
- what must be paid, and when;
- the internal set-up steps and who owns each one;
- what "onboarding complete" means.

Keep the list short. If a document isn't needed for this service, don't ask for it. That's good client experience, and as we'll see below, it's also what data protection law expects.

This checklist becomes the rulebook your automation follows. If your partners can't agree on it, no software will fix the problem. Our [step-by-step guide to automating business processes](/insights/how-to-automate-business-processes-nigeria) covers how to map and simplify a process like this before you automate it.

### Step 2: Replace scattered messages with one intake form

Build one online intake form per service, or one form with sections that appear depending on the service chosen. Tools such as Google Forms, Microsoft Forms, Tally, Jotform or the forms built into a CRM all work. Choose one that:

- lets clients upload files directly, so documents don't arrive on WhatsApp;
- saves every submission to one place (a sheet, a CRM or a database);
- can trigger the next step automatically, either natively or through a connector.

Send the form link the moment a client says yes, with one sentence explaining why it matters: "This form collects everything we need to start, so you won't get repeated requests from us."

If clients still send documents on WhatsApp, and some will, have a simple rule: the staff member uploads them to the client's record the same day and replies with the form link for anything still missing.

### Step 3: Create the client record and folder automatically

When the form is submitted, a connector such as Zapier, Make or n8n (or your CRM's own automation) can do the set-up work nobody enjoys:

- create or update the client record in your CRM or client sheet;
- create a client folder with a consistent name and subfolders (for example, *Client name – Service – Year*, with *KYC*, *Engagement*, *Working papers*);
- move uploaded files into the right subfolder;
- assign an owner and create the onboarding tasks for the team;
- send an internal alert to the owner with a summary and a link to the record.

This is where most firms see the quickest win, because it removes the copying and retyping. Joining forms, storage and your CRM so information flows without re-entry is what we mean by [business systems integration](/what-we-do#integrations).

### Step 4: Send the engagement letter for e-signature

Use a template engagement letter for each service, with merge fields for the client's name, company, fees and scope. When the intake form is complete, the system fills in the template and sends it for signature through an e-signature tool such as DocuSign, Zoho Sign or Dropbox Sign. A partner can review and approve before it goes out; for standard services, many firms let it send automatically.

Are electronic signatures valid in Nigeria? For ordinary commercial agreements, generally yes. Section 17 of the Cybercrimes (Prohibition, Prevention, etc.) Act 2015 recognises electronic signatures for commercial transactions, and in a dispute the burden of proof falls on the person challenging the signature. There are exceptions. A [2023 Mondaq article by Convergence Law Practice](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/contracts-and-commercial-law/1314284/electronic-signatures-in-nigeria) lists documents where electronic signatures are not accepted as binding, including wills and other testamentary documents, birth and death certificates, family-law matters such as divorce and adoption, court orders and cases where the law requires a particular form of signature. A standard engagement letter is not on that list, but if your service involves documents that are, keep a person in charge and take advice.

Set the automation to chase unsigned letters: a reminder after two days, another after five, then an alert to the client owner to call.

### Step 5: Raise the first invoice and confirm payment automatically

Once the letter is signed, the system raises the deposit or first invoice and sends it with a payment link. If you use Paystack, its invoices and payment requests can be created automatically and marked paid when the customer pays. Our guide to [automating payment reminders in Nigeria](/insights/automate-payment-reminders-nigeria) walks through the reminder schedule and how to stop reminders the moment a client pays.

Many Nigerian clients still prefer to pay by bank transfer. Rather than matching alerts by hand, give each client their own account number to pay into, so payments confirm themselves. We explain how in [How to confirm bank transfer payments automatically in Nigeria](/insights/confirm-bank-transfer-payments-automatically-nigeria).

When payment is confirmed, the onboarding record moves to "Paid" and the next step begins. If the firm's policy is "no work before deposit", that rule is now enforced by the system, not by a partner's memory.

### Step 6: Book the kickoff and send a welcome pack

With details, documents, signature and payment in place, send the client a welcome message with:

- who their main contact is (name, photo, WhatsApp or email);
- what happens in the first two weeks;
- a booking link for the kickoff meeting, based on the team's real calendar availability;
- how to send documents in future (the portal or folder link, not WhatsApp).

Calendar tools can handle the booking, confirmation and reminders. Our guide to [automating appointment booking and reminders](/insights/automate-appointment-booking-reminders-nigeria) covers a reminder schedule that cuts no-shows.

### Step 7: Track every new client on one board, and measure

Give the practice manager or owner one view of every client in onboarding, by stage: *Form sent → Details received → Documents complete → Letter signed → Paid → Kickoff booked → Complete*. A simple board in your CRM, or a sheet with a status column, is enough.

Before you switch anything on, record your starting numbers so you can see whether it worked:

- average days from "yes" to work starting;
- staff time spent on onboarding per client;
- how many clients start work with documents still missing;
- days from "yes" to first payment.

Review them after a month. Fix the step where clients stall most.

## What the law expects from your onboarding

Automated onboarding collects personal data and, for many firms, identity documents. Two sets of Nigerian rules matter. This section is general guidance, not legal advice.

**Data protection.** The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 expects businesses to have a lawful basis for processing personal data, to collect only data that is adequate and relevant for the purpose, to keep it secure, and to ensure software providers that process data on their behalf protect it properly. [The Future of Privacy Forum's explainer](https://fpf.org/blog/nigerias-new-data-protection-act-explained/) is a readable overview, and the Nigeria Data Protection Commission's [General Application and Implementation Directive (GAID) 2025](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/NDP-ACT-GAID-2025-MARCH-20TH.pdf) explains how the Act is applied. In practice: ask only for the documents each service needs, limit who can open the KYC folder, use business accounts rather than personal ones, and know where each tool stores your clients' data.

**Anti-money-laundering checks.** Under the Money Laundering (Prevention and Prohibition) Act 2022, certain professions are treated as designated non-financial businesses and professions (DNFBPs), supervised by SCUML. A [guide for accountants published by Learnsignal in October 2026](https://www.learnsignal.com/blog/nigeria-aml-obligations-accountants-money-laundering-act-2022-scuml-guide/) explains that the Act treats licensed professional accountants as DNFBPs, requires firms to identify and verify customers and beneficial owners, and requires due-diligence records to be kept for at least five years after the relationship ends. Enforcement is real: a [December 2025 client alert from Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2025/12/Client-Alert_Nigeria-Commences-Enforcement-Under-Anti-Money-Laundering-Act.pdf) reports that the EFCC arraigned a business before the Federal High Court in Abuja over alleged DNFBP compliance failures, and notes that accounting and consulting firms are now routinely examined for AML gaps. The same alert notes that whether legal practitioners are covered has been contested in the courts, so law firms should take specific advice.

An automated intake can help here rather than hurt. It can make the right ID and ownership documents mandatory for the services that need them, record when they were received and who checked them, and keep them in one controlled folder with a retention date. A person should still review the documents. Automation collects and files; it doesn't make the judgement.

## An illustrative walkthrough: a Lagos accounting practice

*This is an illustrative example, not a client story.*

A six-person accounting and tax practice in Ikeja takes on three or four new SME clients a month. Onboarding used to take two to three weeks, mostly spent chasing documents on WhatsApp.

The practice manager writes one checklist per service and builds an intake form with file uploads. When a client submits it, a no-code connector creates the client in the practice's CRM, builds a Drive folder with *KYC*, *Engagement* and *Working papers* subfolders, files the uploads and assigns the work to a senior accountant. The engagement letter is generated from a template, approved by a partner with one click and sent for e-signature. Once signed, a Paystack invoice goes out automatically, and payment moves the client to "Kickoff". The client receives a welcome message with a booking link.

Missing documents trigger reminders on days two and five, then a task for the senior accountant to call. The partners can see every new client's stage on one board. After a month, the practice manager compares days-to-start and staff hours against her starting numbers before deciding what to automate next.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Asking for everything "just in case".** Long forms put clients off and collect data you then have to protect. Ask for what each service needs.
- **Automating the chasing but not the checking.** A document that has arrived isn't the same as a document that is valid. Keep a human review step for KYC.
- **Sending a generic letter.** Use service-specific templates. The engagement letter sets expectations for the whole relationship.
- **Building it on a personal Gmail or WhatsApp account.** Use business accounts so the process survives staff changes.
- **Removing the personal touch.** Automation should handle the admin so the partner's first call is about the client's business, not missing paperwork.
- **No owner.** Name one person who checks the onboarding board weekly and fixes the automation when a form or template changes.

## When to do it yourself and when to get help

A small firm can build a solid version of this with a form tool, a shared drive, an e-signature tool, a payment provider and a no-code connector. It makes sense to get help when onboarding spans several systems (CRM, practice management, accounting, payments and WhatsApp), when you handle sensitive KYC data at volume, when you want AI to check or extract information from uploaded documents, or when your team simply doesn't have time to build and maintain it.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is client onboarding automation?

It's using software to run the repeatable steps between a client saying yes and the work starting: collecting details and documents, sending the engagement letter, raising the first invoice, setting up the client internally and booking the kickoff. People still handle the judgement calls, such as reviewing documents and agreeing scope.

### What tools do I need to automate client onboarding?

Most small firms need five things: an online form with file uploads, shared storage, an e-signature tool, a payment provider, and either a CRM with built-in automation or a no-code connector such as Zapier, Make or n8n to join them up. Check current pricing, naira billing and data storage locations before you commit.

### Are electronic signatures legal for engagement letters in Nigeria?

For ordinary commercial agreements, generally yes. The Cybercrimes Act 2015 recognises electronic signatures in commercial transactions. Some documents are excluded, including wills, certain family-law matters and court orders, so take advice if your work involves those.

### What KYC documents should I collect from a new business client?

It depends on your service and your obligations. Many firms ask for the company's CAC certificate and status report, TIN, and identification and proof of address for directors and beneficial owners. If your firm is a DNFBP under the Money Laundering Act 2022, check SCUML's requirements and keep the records for the period the Act requires.

### How long should client onboarding take?

There's no fixed standard, but faster is better for both cash flow and first impressions. Measure your current time from "yes" to work starting, automate the steps that cause the longest waits, and track the improvement.

### Can I automate client onboarding on WhatsApp?

Partly. WhatsApp works well for the welcome message, reminders and quick questions, and it's how many Nigerian clients prefer to communicate. Documents and signatures are better handled through a form and an e-signature tool, so they're stored securely in one place.
