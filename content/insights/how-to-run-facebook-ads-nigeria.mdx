Most Nigerian business owners meet Facebook ads through the blue "Boost post" button. You pick an audience, spend a few thousand naira, watch the likes come in, and then wonder why the till looks the same. The problem is rarely Facebook itself. It is that boosting skips most of the decisions that make advertising pay: what the ad is for, how you will pay for it, whether it is legal to run, what happens when someone responds, and how you will know which naira produced a sale.

This guide walks through how to run Facebook ads in Nigeria properly, using Meta Ads Manager, so that the same campaign also runs on Instagram. It covers the setup choices you cannot easily undo, paying from Nigeria, the ARCON approval rules that started being enforced against online sellers in 2026, how Meta's AI now builds most of your campaign for you, and the follow-up and tracking that turn clicks into customers. It is written for owners and marketers who want to run their own ads with confidence.

## Why Facebook ads still matter for Nigerian businesses

Meta's apps are where a large share of Nigerian buying conversations start. Research by the consultancy Public First, commissioned by Meta and [reported by Technext](https://technext24.com/2026/05/24/81-business-meta-expanded-customer-base/) in May 2026, estimated that 14 million Nigerian SMEs used Meta's apps in 2025, and 81% of online businesses surveyed said Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp had helped them sell beyond their immediate area. Treat a Meta-commissioned study with the caution it deserves, but the direction is hard to argue with: your customers are on these apps, and paid ads are the fastest way to reach the ones who don't follow you yet.

What has changed is how much of the work Meta now does for you, and how much of the result depends on what happens after the click.

## Step 1: Set up the accounts you need (and choose your currency carefully)

You need four things before your first ad:

- **A Facebook Page for the business**, not a personal profile. Link your Instagram professional account to it if you want ads on Instagram.
- **A business portfolio (Business Manager)** that owns the Page, the ad account and, later, your tracking. Add at least one other trusted person as an admin so the business is not locked out if one login is lost.
- **An ad account** inside that portfolio.
- **A payment method** that works for that ad account.

The decision people regret most is the ad account's **currency**. It is set when you create the account, and changing it later effectively means starting a new ad account. If you plan to pay in naira, create the account with Nigeria as the country and Nigerian naira as the currency. If you will pay with a dollar card, choose dollars. Decide before you click create.

## Step 2: Choose how you will pay from Nigeria

Payment is where many first campaigns stall. You have two routes.

**Naira billing (prepaid).** According to a 2026 summary of [Meta's Nigeria payment options by Maxme Group](https://www.maxmegroup.com/learn/pay-for-facebook-ads-nigeria), Meta's help centre lists two local manual methods, naira card and NIBSS bank payment, and both require an ad account whose country is Nigeria and whose currency is naira. You add funds first, the ads spend that balance, and they pause when it runs out. Because the charge is processed locally, it does not touch your card's international spending limit.

**Dollar billing (card on file).** On a dollar ad account, Meta charges a card automatically. Most naira debit cards have low or restricted international limits, which is why so many Nigerian advertisers see declined payments and use a virtual dollar card from a fintech instead. If you go this way, check your card's international limit before launch, not after your ads stop.

Whichever route you choose, budget for tax. Meta has charged 7.5% VAT on ads bought by advertisers in Nigeria since January 2022, [as TheCable reported](https://www.thecable.ng/?p=637748) at the time, so ₦100,000 of ads costs more than ₦100,000. If your business is VAT-registered, add your VAT ID in the billing settings so it appears on your receipts.

## Step 3: Get ARCON approval for your ad content

This is the step most Nigerian Facebook ads guides skip, and in 2026 it became expensive to ignore.

The Advertising Regulatory Council of Nigeria (ARCON) requires adverts aimed at Nigerians to be vetted and approved by its Advertising Standards Panel before they are exposed, and a Federal High Court ruling in April 2025 confirmed its authority over social media advertising. According to [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/pro/article/businesses-strained-under-arcons-vetting-regime-as-unapproved-ads-flood-online/), vetting fees run from ₦7,500 for a seven-day review to ₦100,000 for 24-hour fast-track approval. In March 2026, BusinessDay [reported](https://businessday.ng/business-economy/article/businesses-react-as-arcon-slams-n1-million-fine-for-posting-online-ads-without-approval/) that ARCON had started emailing online businesses fines for ads run on Meta platforms without approval, with one vendor describing a ₦3 million demand for three ads.

Two things make this easy to get wrong. First, Meta does not ask for an ARCON certificate before your ad goes live, so Ads Manager approving your ad tells you nothing about ARCON. Second, the process is still largely paper-based. ARCON's own [vetting procedure](https://advertcouncil.gov.ng/_adverts_vetting.php) describes an application to the Director-General, with accelerated options. According to [LawPavilion](https://blog.lawpavilion.com/navigating-nigerias-digital-ad-space-a-deep-dive-into-arcons-vetting-mandate/), SMEs can qualify for lower fees if they meet conditions such as SMEDAN certification or NASME membership.

In practice:

- **Plan your creative a week ahead** and submit a batch of ads together rather than one at a time.
- **Keep the approved version as the version you run.** If you let AI tools rewrite your headline or text (more on this below), the ad people see may no longer match what was approved.
- **Use an ARCON-accredited agency** if you run ads often; many have vetting desks.
- **Take advice for your own situation.** The rules, fees and enforcement are moving, and this guide is not legal advice.

We covered ARCON alongside Meta's AI-content labels in our guide to [using AI to create social media content](/insights/ai-social-media-content-small-business-nigeria).

## Step 4: Boost or Ads Manager?

Boosting a post is fine for one thing: putting more eyes on a post that is already doing well with your followers. For anything you want to measure as sales or leads, use Ads Manager. It gives you the full set of objectives, separate ad sets for different audiences, placement and budget controls, proper reporting, and the tracking setup in Step 9. A useful rule: if you can't say what a "result" is before you spend, you are not ready to spend.

## Step 5: Pick the objective that matches how you actually sell

Meta organises every campaign around six [advertising objectives](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1438417719786914): Awareness, Traffic, Engagement, Leads, App promotion and Sales. The objective tells Meta's system which people to look for, so choosing the wrong one buys you the wrong people cheaply.

Match it to how a customer buys from you:

- **They chat first, then pay by transfer** (fashion, food, gadgets, beauty): use a messaging campaign that opens WhatsApp. Our guide to [Click-to-WhatsApp ads in Nigeria](/insights/click-to-whatsapp-ads-nigeria) covers that setup in detail.
- **They need a call or consultation first** (property, training, professional services, B2B): use **Leads**, usually with an Instant Form. The hard part is the follow-up, which we cover in [how to automate Facebook lead ads follow-up](/insights/automate-facebook-lead-ads-follow-up).
- **They can buy on your website** with Paystack or another checkout: use **Sales**, but only once the website tracking in Step 9 is working.
- **Nobody knows you yet** and you are launching in a new area: Awareness or video Engagement can build a warm audience you then retarget. Judge it on reach and recall, not sales.

Pick one objective per campaign and resist the urge to make one ad do everything.

## Step 6: Let Advantage+ do the targeting, but keep the hard limits

If you last ran ads a few years ago, the campaign screen will look different. In 2025 Meta began defaulting Sales, Leads and App campaigns to Advantage+, its AI-driven setup, and by early 2026 it had merged the old manual and Advantage+ creation flows into one, [according to industry trackers](https://www.r2clickthrough.com/blog/advantage-in-2026-what-changed-for-media-buyers/). New campaigns now start with AI choosing audience, placements (Facebook, Instagram, Messenger and more) and creative enhancements, and you override what you need to.

For most small businesses, that is good news: broad AI targeting often beats the detailed interest stacks that used to be standard. But the AI does not know your business constraints, so set these yourself:

- **Location.** If you only deliver in Lagos and Ogun, do not pay to reach Kano. Location, age and language remain the controls you can set firmly.
- **Age, where the law or your product requires it.**
- **Placements you genuinely cannot use.** Check the small print: for Sales and Leads campaigns, Meta may still spend a small share of budget on a placement you excluded if it predicts better results, unless you untick that option.

Narrowing the audience switches the campaign out of full Advantage+ mode, which is allowed. Do it for real business reasons, not out of habit.

## Step 7: Make creative that is genuinely different, and use AI carefully

With targeting increasingly automated, your creative is now the main lever you control. Meta's own guidance, published in December 2025, says its system groups ads that look or feel alike and treats them as variations of the same creative. Ten versions of the same product photo with different background colours give Meta one idea to work with, not ten.

So build a few distinct concepts rather than many tweaks:

- **A demonstration**: a short phone video of the product in use or the service being delivered.
- **The answer to the main objection**: delivery time, price, authenticity, after-sales support.
- **Proof**: a real customer review (with permission), a before-and-after, or your physical shop.
- **The offer**: price, what is included, where you deliver, how to pay.

Showing your price, or a "from" price, usually brings fewer but better responses than "DM for price", and it helps keep your ads clear of misleading-pricing complaints.

This is where AI saves real time. Use an AI assistant to draft headline and copy variations for different customer types, script a 20-second video, or turn your customer FAQs into ad angles. Meta's built-in Advantage+ creative tools can also generate text variations, expand images and adjust formats automatically. Two cautions for Nigeria: switch off any enhancement that rewrites your words if you need the running ad to match your ARCON-approved version, and check every AI-generated line for claims you cannot prove.

## Step 8: Set a budget that can teach you something

There is no correct budget, and anyone quoting one without knowing your margins is guessing. A better question is: how much can you spend to learn which ad produces a paying customer, and what can you afford to pay for one?

Work it out backwards. If your profit on an average order is ₦8,000, you cannot afford to pay ₦10,000 in ads per sale. Start with a daily budget you can sustain for about two weeks, put it behind two or three genuinely different ads, and avoid editing the campaign every day; frequent changes reset Meta's learning. Meta reviews every ad before it runs, usually within a day, so build that into your launch date as well as ARCON's turnaround.

## Step 9: Track results beyond the click

Ads Manager will report cost per message, cost per lead or cost per click. None of those is the number that pays salaries. You need cost per sale by ad, and in Nigeria that takes a little work because so many sales finish in a WhatsApp chat or with a bank transfer days later.

**Start with a simple sheet.** For every response, record the date, the ad it came from, whether it became a real lead, and whether and how much the customer paid. Each week, divide each ad's spend by its sales. Our guide to [measuring marketing ROI for a small business](/insights/measure-marketing-roi-small-business-nigeria) shows how to do this across all your channels.

**Add Meta's tracking when you have a website.** Install the Meta Pixel and connect the Conversions API, which sends events such as purchases from your server rather than relying on the browser. This is what lets a Sales campaign optimise towards buyers instead of clickers.

**Send offline sales back to Meta when volume justifies it.** Meta retired its separate Offline Conversions API in 2025 and now takes offline events, such as a sale closed by phone or a transfer confirmed in your CRM, through the main Conversions API. When your CRM marks a lead as "paid", an automation can tell Meta, so the system learns what a real customer looks like. This is where marketing becomes an operations job.

## Step 10: Automate what happens after someone responds

The most common reason Facebook ads "don't work" in Nigeria has nothing to do with the ad. A lead fills a form at 9pm and hears nothing until the next afternoon. A WhatsApp chat asks for the price and gets a reply three hours later. By then the customer has bought elsewhere.

You don't need a big system to fix this:

- **Reply within minutes**, with an instant message that answers the most common question and asks one qualifying question.
- **Route every lead to a named person**, not a shared phone that whoever is free picks up.
- **Follow up on a schedule**: same day, next day, and a final nudge a few days later, then stop.
- **Log the outcome** in the same place every time, so the Step 9 numbers are real.

Start with WhatsApp Business quick replies and a shared sheet. When volume grows, connect your ad leads to a CRM with automatic alerts and follow-ups.

## An illustrative example

*This is an illustrative walkthrough, not a client case study or a real result.*

A small skincare brand in Abuja creates a naira ad account and funds it by bank payment, so card limits never interrupt delivery. It prepares three ad concepts (a routine demonstration video, a review from a customer who agreed to be featured, and a price-and-delivery graphic), submits them to ARCON as one batch, and launches only after approval. Text enhancements stay off so the live ads match what was approved.

The campaign uses a messaging objective to WhatsApp, Advantage+ audience, and a location limited to the areas the brand's dispatch rider covers. Every chat gets an instant reply with prices and delivery fees, and the owner logs each conversation in a sheet against the ad it came from. After two weeks, the demonstration video has the highest cost per chat but the lowest cost per sale, so the budget moves there and the next concept is built in that style.

## Common mistakes to avoid

**Choosing the wrong ad account currency** and discovering it only when your card is declined.

**Treating Meta's ad approval as ARCON approval.** They are separate, and only one of them sends fines.

**Judging ads by likes, clicks or cheap messages** instead of cost per sale.

**Making ten near-identical ads** and calling it testing.

**Fiddling with the campaign daily**, so it never finishes learning.

**Paying for leads nobody follows up** quickly, especially at night and at weekends.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does it cost to run Facebook ads in Nigeria?

You set the budget, and Meta charges 7.5% VAT on top for Nigerian advertisers. What matters is your cost per sale, which depends on your product, offer, creative and follow-up. Start with a test budget you can sustain for two weeks and scale only the ads that still leave you a profit per sale.

### Can I pay for Facebook ads with a naira card?

Yes, on an ad account set to Nigeria and naira, where you prepay using naira card or NIBSS bank payment. On a dollar ad account, a naira card is charged internationally and often hits your bank's foreign-spending limit, which is why many advertisers use a virtual dollar card for dollar accounts.

### Do I need ARCON approval to run Facebook ads in Nigeria?

ARCON says adverts aimed at Nigerians need prior approval from its Advertising Standards Panel, including on social media, and it began fining online sellers for unapproved Meta ads in 2026. Meta does not check this for you. Get advice for your specific situation, especially if you run ads regularly.

### Should I boost posts or use Ads Manager?

Boost a post only to give a well-performing post more reach. Use Ads Manager when you want leads or sales, because it gives you the right objectives, controls and reporting.

### Do Facebook ads also run on Instagram?

Yes. Campaigns created in Ads Manager can run across Facebook, Instagram and Meta's other placements, and with Advantage+ placements Meta decides where to spend. Link your Instagram professional account to your Page first.

### Can AI create my Facebook ads?

AI can draft copy, scripts and image variations, and Meta's Advantage+ creative tools can generate variations automatically. You still decide the offer, check every claim, and make sure what runs is what ARCON approved.
