When someone in Lagos, Abuja or Port Harcourt needs a dentist, a caterer, a mechanic or a training centre, a lot of them do the same thing: they search on their phone and look at the businesses that come up on the map. What they see there, the photos, the star rating, the opening hours and a button to call or chat, comes from a Google Business Profile.

Google is where that search happens in Nigeria. StatCounter's figures put Google's share of search in Nigeria at [over 98% in mid-2026](https://gs.statcounter.com/search-engine-market-share/all/nigeria). So if your profile is missing, unverified or half-finished, you're invisible at the exact moment a ready-to-buy customer is looking.

This guide shows you how to set up and optimise your Google Business Profile in Nigeria, step by step: verification, the details that help you show up, reviews, the WhatsApp button, and how to tell whether the profile is actually bringing in customers. It also covers the part most guides skip: the simple automations that keep your profile working without someone remembering to do it.

## What a Google Business Profile does for a Nigerian business

A Google Business Profile (it used to be called Google My Business) is a free listing that appears in Google Search and Google Maps. A complete profile shows your name, category, location or service area, hours, phone number, website, photos and reviews, and lets people call you, get directions or open your website with one tap.

For many small businesses, it does three jobs at once:

- **It gets you found** for searches like "physiotherapist in Surulere" or "event caterer near me", including by people who have never heard of you.
- **It builds trust before the first conversation.** Reviews and real photos answer the question every customer has: are these people legit?
- **It sends enquiries** straight to your phone, your WhatsApp or your website.

You don't need a website to have a profile, though a good one helps (more on that below). Our guide on [how to do SEO for a small business in Nigeria](/insights/seo-for-small-business-nigeria) covers the website side, and the two work best together.

## How Google decides who shows up

Google is open about the broad rules. Its help page on [improving your local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) says local results are based mainly on three things:

- **Relevance:** how well your profile matches what the person searched for. Complete, accurate information helps here.
- **Distance:** how far your business is from the searcher, or from the location in their search.
- **Prominence:** how well known your business is, online and off. Google says more reviews and positive ratings can improve your local ranking, and that your position in normal web results also counts.

You can't move your shop closer to every customer, so the work is on relevance and prominence. Everything in this guide serves one or both.

## Before you start: check you're eligible

Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) allow a profile if you either have a physical location customers can visit, or you travel to customers where they are. That has a few practical consequences:

- **Online-only businesses don't qualify.** If customers never meet you in person, a profile isn't the right tool.
- **Home-based businesses that visit customers** (a mobile mechanic, a home cleaning service, a caterer who delivers) can create a *service-area* profile. Google says you should hide your home address and set the areas you serve instead.
- **Virtual offices aren't eligible.** A rented mailing address you don't operate from won't do, and a co-working desk only counts if it has signage, staff and receives customers during business hours.
- **One profile per business location.** Duplicates cause problems and can get profiles suspended.

## Step 1: Create or claim your profile

Search for your business name on Google Maps first. Someone (a customer, a former employee, Google itself) may already have created a listing. If it exists, claim it. If it doesn't, create one at business.google.com.

Use a Google account the business controls, not a staff member's personal email. When that person leaves, you don't want your profile leaving with them. Once you're verified, you can add other people as managers so nobody has to share a password.

## Step 2: Get verified

Verification is how Google confirms you're the real owner. According to Google's [verification help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242), the methods (phone or text, email, video recording, or sometimes a live video call) are chosen automatically by Google based on your business type, location and other details. You can't pick one that isn't offered, and you may be asked for more than one.

If video is offered, Google recommends it. For a storefront business, a good verification video usually shows:

- the street and a nearby landmark or street sign, so Google can place you
- your signage with the business name as it appears on your profile
- proof that you run the place, such as opening the shop, the equipment you use, or your business documents

After you submit, Google says the review can take up to five business days. A common reason for rejection is a mismatch: the name on your sign doesn't match the name on your profile, or the address is vague. Fix those first, then try again.

## Step 3: Fill in the details that help you show up

This is where relevance is won or lost. Work through each section carefully.

### Business name: your real name, nothing else

Use the name on your signage, invoices and stationery. Google's guidelines don't allow marketing taglines, locations, service descriptions or keywords in the name, and breaking that rule can get a profile suspended. "Mama Chi's Kitchen" is fine. "Mama Chi's Kitchen – Best Jollof Rice Lekki" is not. It's tempting because it sometimes works for a while, but it's not worth risking the whole profile.

### Categories: fewer and more specific

Your primary category matters a lot. Google advises choosing the fewest categories that describe your core business, as specific as possible, and using the test "this business *is* a…", not "this business *has* a…". A dental clinic is a "Dentist" or "Dental clinic", not "Teeth whitening service" plus "X-ray lab" plus "Pharmacy". Add secondary categories only for things your business genuinely is.

### Address, service area and map pin

Write your address the way a driver would find you. Google's guidelines say landmarks and cross-streets can be included where the official street address doesn't pinpoint the business, which describes a lot of Nigerian addresses. If your address doesn't show correctly, drag the map pin to your exact entrance. Service-area businesses should clear the address and list the towns or LGAs they cover.

### Hours, phone and website

Keep hours accurate, including special hours for public holidays. Use a phone number that's answered during those hours. Google asks for a number under the business's direct control, and prefers a local number to a central call centre.

For the website field, link your own site, or the most relevant page of it. Google's guidelines don't allow links or numbers that redirect people somewhere other than the business itself, and they specifically mention pages created on social media sites. We'll add tracking to this link in Step 7.

### Business description

Explain what you do, who you serve, where, and what makes you different, in plain language. Google gives you a limited number of characters (750 at the time of writing), so put the most important information first. Its rules don't allow links, prices or time-limited promotions in the description. Save offers for posts.

AI tools are genuinely useful here. Give an assistant your services, your areas, your opening hours and two or three things customers praise you for, and ask for a few versions of a 700-character description. Then edit it so it sounds like you and contains nothing that isn't true. Google rejects descriptions with irrelevant or gimmicky content, so keep it factual.

### Services, products and attributes

Add every main service with a short description, and products if you sell them. Then fill in attributes that apply (such as wheelchair access or online appointments). These help Google match you to specific searches and help customers decide.

## Step 4: Add photos that look like the real place

Photos answer questions before people ask them. Upload your logo and a cover photo, then real pictures: the outside of your building so people recognise it on arrival, your team at work, your products, your waiting area. Avoid stock images.

Add new photos regularly rather than 50 on day one. A steady flow shows the business is active, and it gives returning visitors something new.

## Step 5: Turn on the WhatsApp button

In July 2024 Google [removed its own chat feature](https://support.google.com/business/answer/14919056) from Business Profiles. In its place, eligible claimed and verified profiles can add a **Chat** option that links to WhatsApp or text messaging. Google's [help page on messaging](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16751381) gives the path: *Edit profile → Contact → Chat → choose WhatsApp or Text message*.

Two things to know:

- **Availability varies.** Google describes it as a feature for eligible profiles, so check your own. If you don't see the option, keep your phone number prominent and mention WhatsApp in your description.
- **Pick one.** Google says that if you add both WhatsApp and a text-message number, customers only see the text-message option.

When you add your WhatsApp link, use a click-to-chat link with a short pre-filled message, such as *"Hi, I found you on Google and I'd like to ask about…"*. When chats arrive starting with that line, you know exactly where they came from. ## Step 6: Get more reviews, the right way

Reviews are the part of your profile you can't fill in yourself, and they carry a lot of weight. BrightLocal's [Local Consumer Review Survey 2026](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/), a survey of 1,002 US adults, found that 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses, 74% care only about reviews from the last three months, and 47% won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews. That's US data, not Nigerian, but the direction is clear: you need a steady flow of recent reviews, not a burst once a year.

### Get your review link and QR code

Google's help page on [requesting reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815) explains how: open your profile, select **Read reviews**, then **Get more reviews**. You can copy a link that opens the review form directly, or download a QR code. Google notes the QR code can currently only be generated in a computer browser, not on a phone.

Print the QR code at your counter, on receipts and on delivery packaging. Put the link in thank-you messages and email signatures.

### Know the rules before you ask

Google's [Maps user-generated content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) is strict, and breaking it can get reviews removed. Merchants must not:

- offer incentives, such as discounts, free items or payment, for a review, or for changing or removing a negative one
- discourage negative reviews or **only ask happy customers** (often called "review gating")
- pressure customers to leave a review while they're still on your premises, or ask them to include specific content
- set staff targets for a number of reviews, or ask staff to get reviews that mention them by name

What's allowed is simple: ask customers for an honest review of a genuine experience, with nothing attached.

### Automate the ask

The most reliable way to get recent reviews is to ask every customer at the same point in their journey, automatically. Good moments are when a job is marked complete, an order is delivered, or a payment comes in.

If you've already automated payments, this is a small addition. In our guide to [automating payment reminders in Nigeria](/insights/automate-payment-reminders-nigeria), the system sends a thank-you when a payment is confirmed. Add one line with your review link to that message, sent to every paying customer, and reviews start arriving without anyone having to remember. Because everyone gets the same request, you also stay on the right side of Google's rule against asking only happy customers.

### Reply to every review

The same BrightLocal survey found that 89% of consumers expect businesses to respond to reviews, and that 50% are put off by templated or generic replies. Reply to every review, positive or negative, and make each one specific.

AI can help you draft replies quickly, but read and personalise every one before posting. For a negative review, acknowledge the problem, don't argue in public, and move the conversation to a private channel. Never include a customer's personal details in a reply.

## Step 7: Measure what the profile brings in

Without measurement, you can't tell whether all this effort is producing customers. Use three layers.

**1. The Performance report.** In your profile, the Performance section shows how many people interacted with it and how. Google's [performance help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9918094) lists metrics such as calls (taps on your call button), website clicks, directions requests, messages and bookings. Only the metrics that apply to your business appear. Look at monthly trends, not single days.

**2. Tag your website link.** Clicks from your profile often show up in analytics as "direct" traffic. Add tracking labels (UTM parameters) to the website link on your profile, for example:

`https://www.yourbusiness.com/?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp`

In Google Analytics 4, visits from your profile then appear under that campaign name in the traffic acquisition report.

**3. Ask, and record the answer.** The WhatsApp pre-filled message from Step 5 tells you which chats came from Google. For calls, ask new customers how they found you and log it in your CRM or a simple sheet. Then compare the number of enquiries from Google with the number that turned into paying customers. ## A real example of what consistent work can do

BrightLocal, a local SEO software and services company, publishes a [case study](https://www.brightlocal.com/case-studies/anthem-self-storage/) about Anthem Self Storage, a storage facility in Everett, Washington, in the US. The work included adding relevant secondary categories and detailed service descriptions to the Google Business Profile, matching those categories to service pages on the website, posting regular updates and adding original photos each month, and putting pricing and availability on the main service page.

BrightLocal reports that over six months, clicks rose from 109 to 294, impressions from 7.91K to 32.1K and its Map Pack visibility from 34% to 44%, and that the facility's occupancy rose from the low 70s to 83%. This is a vendor's account of its own client in a different market, so treat it as an example of the approach rather than a forecast. The lesson that does travel: the gains came from steady, unglamorous work on accurate information, real photos and an active profile, not from tricks.

## An illustrative walkthrough: a dental clinic in Ikeja

*This is an illustrative example, not a TechieBreed client.*

A two-chair dental clinic in Ikeja has a profile someone created years ago. The name includes "Best Dentist Ikeja", the hours are out of date, there are six reviews (the newest from 2023) and no photos except a blurry signboard.

Here's what a month of work looks like:

1. **Week 1:** the owner claims the profile with the clinic's own Google account, verifies by video, removes the keywords from the name, sets "Dentist" as the primary category, and fixes the hours and map pin.
2. **Week 2:** they add every service with a short description, write a 700-character description with help from an AI assistant, and upload real photos of the building, reception and treatment room.
3. **Week 3:** they add the WhatsApp chat link with a pre-filled message, tag the website link, and print the review QR code for the reception desk.
4. **Week 4 onwards:** after each appointment is paid, an automated WhatsApp message thanks the patient and includes the review link. The receptionist replies to every review within a day, and the owner checks the Performance report on the first Monday of each month.

None of these steps is complicated. The difference comes from doing all of them and keeping them going.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Keyword-stuffing your business name.** It risks suspension of the whole profile.
- **Creating a second profile** to "cover" another area or replace one you lost access to. Recover the original instead.
- **Offering a discount for reviews,** or only sending the review link to customers you know are happy.
- **Using a staff member's personal account** as the only owner.
- **Setting it up once and forgetting it.** Out-of-date hours, old photos and unanswered reviews all tell customers the business may not be active.
- **Measuring views instead of customers.** A thousand profile views mean little if nobody calls.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Google Business Profile free in Nigeria?

Yes. Creating and managing a Google Business Profile is free. You may choose to pay for Google Ads or for help managing the profile, but the profile itself costs nothing.

### Can I create a Google Business Profile without a shop?

Yes, if you travel to customers, as a service-area business. Hide your address and list the areas you serve. If your business is entirely online and customers never meet you in person, you aren't eligible.

### How long does Google Business Profile verification take?

Google says that after you complete the verification steps, its review can take up to five business days. The method (video, phone, text or email) is chosen by Google and depends on your business.

### Why isn't my business showing on Google Maps?

The usual reasons are that the profile isn't verified, the information is incomplete or inaccurate, the category is wrong, or there are stronger competitors closer to the searcher. Work through the steps above, then focus on getting recent reviews.

### Can I ask customers for 5-star reviews?

You can ask customers for an honest review, but you can't ask for a particular rating, suggest what to write, offer anything in return, or only ask customers you think are happy. Google's policy prohibits all of these.

### Can I add WhatsApp to my Google Business Profile?

If the Chat option appears under Contact when you edit your profile, yes. Google offers it to eligible profiles that are claimed and verified. Choose WhatsApp and add your click-to-chat link.
